## The stars

- **Bright stars (`@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars`)**: the Yale Bright Star Catalogue, D. Hoffleit and W. H. Warren Jr. (1991), "The Bright Star Catalogue, 5th Revised Edition (Preliminary Version)", Astronomical Data Center (NASA Goddard Space Flight Center) and the Centre de Données astronomiques de Strasbourg, catalogue V/50, as NASA's HEASARC serves it (table bsc5p, https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/W3Browse/star-catalog/bsc5p.html, file heasarc_bsc5p.tdat.gz, read 2026-11-08; a US government source), the 5,080 stars to visual magnitude 6.0, written out as numbers by `scripts/make-star-data.py` into `src/data/stars.ts`. The catalogue's own documentation calls it public domain and the Astronomical Data Center asks that it and the authors be acknowledged, which this line does; there is no formal licence text, so this footing is informal. **Confirmation requested 2026-11-09:** John Morris wrote to NASA's HEASARC on 2026-11-10 to confirm these terms. Until the answer comes, nothing is removed; the stars are the package's core, so no fallback is built.
  - **Spectral types and parallaxes of the bright stars**: the spectral type of each star is the Bright Star Catalogue's own string, whole (`M1-2Ia-Iab`). Its parallax is, where the Yale General Catalogue of Trigonometric Stellar Parallaxes (the source credited under Nearby stars below, same file, same terms) holds the star and gives a positive parallax, that catalogue's weighted mean parallax and standard error (matched by Henry Draper number, with the V magnitudes agreeing to 1.0; the components of a pair take the pair's parallax): 2,329 stars. Otherwise it is the Bright Star Catalogue's parallax to a whole milliarcsecond with no error (71 stars), or the catalogue's dynamical parallax (code D, 163 stars), which is kept in preference to a trigonometric one. Counts as of 2026-11-09, by `scripts/make-star-data.py`.
  - **Hipparcos new reduction, not used (read 2026-11-09)**: van Leeuwen (2007), VizieR catalogue I/311, would give better parallaxes but is ESA's. ESA's Hipparcos page (https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/hipparcos/catalogues) says: "The Hipparcos and Tycho Catalogues are distributed under the CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO licence", with the credit "Credit: ESA". Non-commercial terms cannot be passed on under MIT, ESA's page for the new reduction states no other terms, and CDS's I/311 ReadMe could not be read (the host refused the connection), so the new reduction is treated as under the same non-commercial licence and none of its values, nor its HIP numbers, are read.
- **Nearby stars (`@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/nearby`)**: the stars that the Yale General Catalogue of Trigonometric Stellar Parallaxes holds and the bright stars do not, written out as numbers by `scripts/make-nearby-data.py` into `src/data/nearby.ts`. The build found 8,054 stars with a parallax in the catalogue (8,268 records, components and unresolved sums share a number); 2,489 are among the 5,080 bright stars (2,482 matched by HD number, 7 by position within 15 arc-seconds and magnitude within 1.0; 8 of the 2,489 are worth a second look) and **5,565 are new and written out**. Of the new: 4,352 have a 2MASS position and 1,213 keep the catalogue position (flagged coarse); 1,029 are flagged ambiguous; 496 have a parallax of 0 or less (excluded from 3D). Counts as of 2026-11-08.
  - **Yale GCTP**: W. F. van Altena, J. T. Lee and E. D. Hoffleit (1995), "The General Catalogue of Trigonometric Stellar Parallaxes, Fourth Edition", Yale University Observatory, catalogue I/238A of the Centre de Données astronomiques de Strasbourg (CDS) VizieR service, file `picat.dat` (1,173,412 bytes, SHA-256 `3b483e9ddf76e2fa9860adf2d25aae79ee257de428284c3351e5e81e402727a6`) from VizieR's Harvard mirror (https://vizier.cfa.harvard.edu/ftp/cats/I/238A/, read 2026-11-08). **No licence is stated**: the ReadMe has no licence, copyright or terms sentence, and VizieR's own page says commercial use of its data "is subject to rules depending of the origin". By this package's rule that data with no licence is used with credit, it is used here with the credit above, as measured numbers (parallaxes, positions, magnitudes), not the printed book or its text. **It is removed on request**: write to the repository's owner and the stars come out of the next release. The positions are B1900.0 in the FK4 system; they are carried to J2000 ICRS with astropy's FK4 to FK5 transformation after the catalogue's own proper motion has moved them. The identifiers `GJ` (Gliese-Jahreiss) and `HD` are numbers only; no data of those catalogues is read. **Confirmation requested 2026-11-09:** John Morris wrote to the CDS on 2026-11-10 to confirm these terms. Until the answer comes, nothing is removed; a build without it (`/nearby` left out, the bright stars back on the Bright Star Catalogue's own parallaxes) is being made ready (MODULES-779).
  - **2MASS**: the modern positions are from the Two Micron All Sky Survey Point Source Catalog (Cutri et al. 2003), read on 2026-11-08 through NASA/IPAC's Infrared Science Archive TAP service (https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/TAP, table `fp_psc`), in batches by `scripts/make-nearby-data.py`; the designation and the position error of each source are kept. IRSA lists it as a US government work (https://www.usa.gov/government-works) and IPAC asks for this acknowledgement, which this line gives: "This publication makes use of data products from the Two Micron All Sky Survey, which is a joint project of the University of Massachusetts and the Infrared Processing and Analysis Center/California Institute of Technology, funded by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration and the National Science Foundation."
  - **Not used**: Gaia, Hipparcos and Tycho values (CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO). Their catalogue numbers are not read either.

  - **APASS.** UCAC4 carries the AAVSO Photometric All-Sky Survey's V and B magnitudes (DR6). The magnitude of a faint star and its colour are APASS's, and VizieR's ReadMe for APASS (II/336, https://vizier.cfa.harvard.edu/ftp/cats/II/336/ReadMe) asks: "This publication makes use of data products from the AAVSO Photometric All Sky Survey (APASS).   - **APASS DR10, for the bright end (MODULES-786, built 2026-11-10; free use with credit).** UCAC4's V of its stars brighter than about 9.5 is APASS DR6's, which saturates, and runs 0.4 to 1.2 magnitudes too faint (`docs/research/gap-6.5-8.md`; the guide has the figures). Where APASS DR10 holds the star (V from 7.0 to 10.2, south of declination +20.0, V error 0.3 or less, within 5 arc-seconds of a star of the layer), the tile's V is DR10's, and its colour is DR10's B-V where both errors are under 0.1: 227,998 stars took DR10's V and 155,858 its colour. DR10 is read through the GAVO Data Center's TAP service (https://dc.g-vo.org/tap, table `apass.dr10`, read 2026-11-09 and 2026-11-10; columns `id`, `ra`, `dec`, `ra_error`, `dec_error`, `nobs_v`, `mag_v`, `err_mag_v`, `nobs_b`, `mag_b`, `err_mag_b`); the sentences relied on and both credit lines are under "APASS DR10 (the other fill ...)" below, and the credit applies: "This research was made possible through the use of the AAVSO Photometric All-Sky Survey (APASS), funded by the Robert Martin Ayers Sciences Fund and NSF AST-1412587." DR10 has its bright stars to declination +20.5 and about a tenth of them beyond (measured, `docs/guides/faint-stars.md`), so none north of +20.0 is corrected; the tile format has no room for a mark on each star, so `faint-tiles/index.json` says per tile how many stars took DR10's V (`apassV.tiles`). SAO's magnitudes are not used (`--correct apass,sao` would, and waits for NASA HEASARC's answer, MODULES-787).
  - **2MASS, to tell ghosts from stars (MODULES-769, built 2026-11-10; US government work).** UCAC4 holds detections in the halo of a bright star that are not stars. A kept star within 30 arc-seconds x 10^(0.2 x (5.5 - V)) of a bright star of visual magnitude V (at most 120 arc-seconds) is dropped unless a 2MASS Point Source Catalog source within 2 arc-seconds confirms it (a source flagged for persistence, diffraction spike, stripe or halo does not, nor does the bright star's own source seen again). The 857 stars in such halos were looked up through NASA/IPAC's IRSA TAP service (https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/TAP, table `fp_psc`, read 2026-11-10; columns `designation`, `ra`, `dec`, `j_m`, `ph_qual`, `cc_flg`, the positions going up as an uploaded table): 182 are confirmed and kept, 675 are dropped. 2MASS is as under Nearby stars (IRSA lists it as a US government work; IPAC asks for the acknowledgement quoted there, and this line gives it): "This publication makes use of data products from the Two Micron All Sky Survey, which is a joint project of the University of Massachusetts and the Infrared Processing and Analysis Center/California Institute of Technology, funded by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration and the National Science Foundation." Only UCAC4's own object type 1 ("near an overexposed star") is not used as a sign of a ghost: 3,873 of 4,000 stars of that type are confirmed by the same test, as 3,835 of 4,000 ordinary stars are.
  - **What is read.** Position at J2000.0 (`ra`, `dec`), APASS V (`vmag`) and B (`bmag`), UCAC's own model magnitude (`momag`, 579 to 642 nm, between V and R), and the flags `ojt` (object type), `tf` (the FK6-Hipparcos-Tycho source flag), `ver` and `ber` (the V and B errors), `dsf`. No proper motion or parallax is read; APASS DR10 and 2MASS are read as the two entries before this one say. The magnitude is APASS V where the record has one that is APASS's own, else UCAC's model magnitude (of the rows not dropped as object type 4, 6 or 7, 4,308,852 had the first, 401,001 the second); it is stored to 0.05. The colour is APASS B-V where both are APASS's own, else none (211,217 of the 2,461,864 stars have none).
  - **Not Hipparcos, Tycho or Gaia.** UCAC4 copies in values from the Hipparcos and Tycho-2 catalogues (ESA's, CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO, see above) and from the FK6, "for all bright stars not observed with the astrograph" and where its own measure was poor ("the FK6/Hipparcos/Tycho-2 astrometric data are used instead of the UCAC observational data", `ucac4doc.txt`). Every such row is dropped, by UCAC4's own flags: object type 6 (position and proper motion substituted from FK6, Hipparcos or Tycho-2; 34,472 rows), object type 7 (a supplement star from them with no CCD data; 4,215 rows) and object type 4 (external high-proper-motion data used instead of UCAC's own, whose source the catalogue does not name; 4,932 rows). Where the source flag `tf` is not 0 and the APASS error is 0, the V (or B) in the record is Tycho's Vt (or Bt), by the catalogue's own words ("apasm(2) < 20000 and apase(2) = 0"), and is not used: 72,164 stars were measured by UCAC's model magnitude instead. A record whose `tf` is not 0 but which is none of the above has UCAC's own position and magnitude and a cross-reference to Tycho-2 only; John chose on 2026-11-09 to keep them (2,027,855 of the 2,462,539 stars before the ghosts were dropped), and the guide says so. The weighted-mean positions are on the ICRS "as represented by the Hipparcos / Tycho-2 catalogs", which is the reference frame and no value.
  - **Not the bright stars twice.** 45 rows within 20 arc-seconds of one of the 5,080 bright stars and within 1.5 magnitudes of it are dropped. Of 4,753,472 rows read, 2,247,269 were fainter than 12.0 (the download takes a margin of half a magnitude), 43,619 were dropped as above, and 675 as ghosts (the 2MASS entry above): 516 of the 5,080 bright stars had a faint star within 20 arc-seconds before, 21 have now.
  - **What the layer is not.** It is thin brighter than about magnitude 9, because the stars UCAC4 only copied are the bright ones (7,838 stars between 6.5 and 8, where the sky holds about 33,600, after the APASS DR10 magnitudes brought stars that UCAC4 gave too faint up into it; 3,138 before): the Bright Star Catalogue to 6.0 and this layer do not make a complete catalogue between 6 and 9. North of declination +20.0 the magnitudes of UCAC4's bright stars are not corrected (about 100,000 stars of 8.5 to 10), and run about 0.4 too faint at 8.5 to 9.5 by comparison with SAO (the guide has the figures). The guide shows the counts. A fill of the gap is built and on file (next entry) and is not merged.

The raw downloads are kept outside the repository (`~/Projects/backups/hoshi-stars/2026-11-09-sao-raw/` and `2026-11-09-apass-2mass-raw/`); the sentences relied on were read on 2026-11-09.
  - **SAO Star Catalog (the SAO fill; confirmation requested 2026-11-09, not used until it comes)**: the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory Star Catalog (SAO Staff 1966), the Astronomical Data Center's J2000 version (VizieR I/131A; positions and proper motions on the FK5 by Clayton A. ## The constellation figures

- **Figures (`@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/lines`)**: our own. For each of the 88 constellations the stars to join were chosen for this package from the positions and brightness of the Yale stars and written as lists of Bright Star Catalogue (HR) numbers in `src/data/lines.ts`. No figure is copied or transcribed from a book, a star atlas, Sky & Telescope, H. A. ## Sky cultures

- **Sky cultures (`@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/culture`, `.../cultures/western`, `.../cultures/japanese`, `.../cultures/chinese`)**: which stars make each figure is a fact; the data are facts typed or read from the sources below, and the lines are our own joins. No file of Stellarium's sky cultures (GPL, CC BY-SA), no Wikipedia text (CC BY-SA) and no source with non-commercial or share-alike terms is read. Read 2026-11-09 unless said otherwise.
  - **The traditional Chinese sky (`src/data/culture-chinese.ts`, by `scripts/make-culture-data.py`)**: Wikidata (https://www.wikidata.org), CC0 1.0 (https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing: "All structured data (i.e. the main, Property, Lexeme, and EntitySchema namespaces) is released into the public domain under Creative Commons Zero."), through its SPARQL endpoint https://query.wikidata.org/sparql. ## The Milky Way

- **Milky Way brightness (`@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/milky-way`)**: a grid of 360 by 120 numbers of 0 to 31 (1 degree of galactic longitude by 0.5 degree of latitude, within 30 degrees of the plane) in `src/data/milky-way.ts`, written by `scripts/make-milky-way-data.py` from three sources. No image of the sky is read, copied or traced.
  - **Star counts: the SAO Star Catalog** (Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory Star Catalog, 258,944 stars to about visual magnitude 9.3; catalogue I/131A at the CDS), as NASA's HEASARC serves it (table `sao`, https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/W3Browse/star-catalog/sao.html, file https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/FTP/heasarc/dbase/tdat_files/heasarc_sao.tdat.gz, read 2026-11-08). **No licence is stated for this catalogue: it is used with credit to the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory and NASA's HEASARC, and removed on request.** Only the stars of visual magnitude 7.0 to 9.0 are counted, on an equal-area grid, and only the counts (a handful of smoothed numbers per square degree) are kept: no star of the catalogue is in the package. The counts show where the Milky Way's stars are thick and, where the catalogue falls short of its surroundings, where near dust (the Coalsack) hides the stars behind it.
  - **Dust: the IRAS Sky Survey Atlas, 100 micron** (NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive; IRAS was a NASA, Dutch and British mission), as NASA's SkyView serves it (https://skyview.gsfc.nasa.gov, survey `IRAS 100`, read 2026-11-08), which labels the survey "Copyright: Public Domain" and its provenance "NASA IPAC/Jet Propulsion Laboratory". Where the atlas has unobserved strips, **COBE DIRBE's zodi-subtracted mission-average 100 micron map** (the COBE Team, NASA Goddard; SkyView survey `COBE DIRBE/ZSMA`, labelled "Copyright: Public Domain") fills them, on a scale matched in the plane. The map's glow compared with the glow around it says where the dust lanes are (the Great Rift, the Cygnus Rift); it is not an image of them. "IRAS" in SkyView is the ISSA atlas, not the Schlegel, Finkbeiner and Davis map nor IRIS, which others derived from it and which are not used.
  - **The bulge and the disk: our own figures.** The bright core toward Sagittarius is a Gaussian some 8 degrees along the plane and 3.5 across, and the glow along the plane follows an exponential disk (scale length 2.6 kpc, the Sun 8.2 kpc from the centre) added up along each line of sight: round, published values of the Galaxy's size, not a copy of any map or model. The counts change the disk's glow only by how much each direction is richer or poorer than its neighbourhood.
  - **Galactic frame**: the IAU's (north galactic pole at right ascension 192.85948 and declination 27.12825 degrees, and the north celestial pole at galactic longitude 122.93192 degrees, J2000), written as formulas.
  - **Not used**: any photograph or drawing of the Milky Way (ESO's panorama, NASA's and ESA's pictures and Gaia's colour map were looked at, nowhere in the data or code, only to judge the result by eye); Planck's maps; the Schlegel, Finkbeiner and Davis dust map; Stellarium's textures (GPL) and anyone's artwork.

## Your sky

- **Horizon view (`@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/sky`)**: written from the formulas in J. Meeus, "Astronomical Algorithms", 2nd ed. (Willmann-Bell, 1998): Greenwich mean sidereal time (the IAU 1982 expression, chapter 12), equatorial to horizontal coordinates (chapter 13), atmospheric refraction (Saemundsson's formula, chapter 16) and precession from J2000 (the IAU 1976 constants, chapter 21). The formulas are mathematics and the worked examples (12.a, 12.b, 13.b, 21.b) are used only as test values; no book text or library code is copied.
- **UTC to Terrestrial Time (`horizontalFrom`)**: the leap seconds (TAI - UTC, 10 s in 1972 to 37 s in 2017, a table of facts published by the IERS and the BIPM) plus 32.184 s; before 1972, delta T by the polynomial fits of F. Espenak and J. Meeus ("Polynomial Expressions for Delta T", NASA Eclipse Web Site, 2006), written out as formulas in `src/time-scale.ts`; no code is copied. The test compares the leap-second part with wakusei-planets' conversion, a dev-only second opinion.
- **Another planet**: the convention is that of the IAU Working Group on Cartographic Coordinates and Rotational Elements (pole right ascension and declination, and W = W0 + rate d), as in Archinal et al., Celestial Mechanics and Dynamical Astronomy 130, 22 (2018). The tests type in the 2015 Mars values from that report; the package itself holds no planet data.

The numbers used are a visual magnitude and a major axis in arc-minutes for each object, which are measurements, not expression. Wikidata's V magnitude for 14 Messier objects is more than a magnitude from it (the central star's for the planetary nebulae M27, M57, M76 and M97, and values more than a magnitude from HEASARC's for M10, M14, M19, M20, M22, M49, M71, M84, M88 and M91); HEASARC's value is used for all 108 it lists, Wikidata's for M73 and M102. HEASARC's size is used for the 107 Messier objects it gives one for (Wikidata has a size for only 6; M40, M73 and M102 have none). **Confirmation requested 2026-11-09:** John Morris wrote to NASA's HEASARC and Sky & Telescope (Sky Publishing) on 2026-11-10 to confirm these terms. Fouqué, 1991, Springer; CDS catalogue VII/155, VizieR; HEASARC table `rc3`, https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/W3Browse/galaxy-catalog/rc3.html, file `heasarc_rc3.tdat.gz`, last modified 2020-09-28). **Confirmation requested 2026-11-09:** RC3 states no licence and its terms are to be confirmed; until the answer comes it is kept, and it is removable with one flag: `python3 -I scripts/make-deep-sky-shapes.py --no-rc3` builds the shapes from NED alone (and `test/shapes-build.test.ts` runs both builds). Used for galaxies: the mean log of the major isophotal diameter at 25 B-mag/arcsec^2 (`log_d25`, in 0.1 arc-minute), the log of the ratio of the major to the minor diameter (`log_r25`) and the position angle of the major axis, degrees north through east (`position_angle`). A galaxy is matched by its NGC or IC number (the table's name or either alternative name) and a match is kept only if RC3's position is within the larger of 6 arc-minutes and 0.6 of the diameter of Wikidata's: 31 are refused (nearly all are NGC numbers that Wikidata merges with a neighbour's). 7,013 galaxies get their shape from RC3 (4,734 with an angle: RC3 gives none for a round galaxy, and where it gives a ratio of 1 to a few per cent none is drawn); RC3 is tried before NED.

  - **What is kept**: the answer held 22,617 rows, 19,309 distance statements for 6,837 items. 11,334 statements are ranked deprecated (Wikidata's mark for a value known to be wrong or withdrawn) and are not used. 627 more are under 20 kiloparsecs and are not used either: the nearest galaxy of the NGC and IC lists is the Small Magellanic Cloud at 60 kiloparsecs, so a smaller distance belongs to a star cluster or nebula that the deep-sky table types as a galaxy (NGC 6469 at 550 parsecs, NGC 6535 at 6.9 kiloparsecs). The units are parsec (Q12129), kiloparsec (Q11929860), megaparsec (Q3773454), light-year (Q531), kilometre and metre, the items checked against Wikidata's English labels on 2026-11-10; no statement was in another unit. Each item is matched to a galaxy of the table by its catalogue codes (P528: M, NGC, IC); three items whose codes name two galaxies (Q1037522, Q1168518, Q3711223) are left out. A galaxy with several statements keeps the median distance in parsecs, the lowest, the highest and the count. The error is half the width of the bounds a statement gives (1,178 galaxies have one; the median of those it has), and is `null` otherwise. `disagree` is set when the highest is more than 1.2 times the lowest (22 galaxies). 6,202 of the 10,282 galaxies (40 of the Messier galaxies) have a distance. Each row carries its Wikidata item number or numbers, so any value can be traced.
  - **The method is "compiled, method not stated"** for every one of them: Wikidata's "determination method" (P459) is on 2 of the 19,309 statements, and most statements name the paper they were taken from, not how it measured. The errors are statistical, and where there are several values they differ from one another by more than any one error says; the demo shows the range and the word "disagree".
  - **What this does not prove**: Wikidata's editors have copied many of these values from other compilations (probably NED-D and HyperLEDA, which is not visible in the data). CC0 is Wikidata's own dedication and does not show that the upstream compilation was free to copy; this package passes on what Wikidata passes on, as it does for the objects themselves. If a compilation's owner objects, the values drawn from it go.

- **Deep-sky pictures (`@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/deep-sky/pictures`, the index in `src/data/deep-sky-pictures.ts`, the files in the repository's `pictures/`, by `scripts/make-deep-sky-pictures.py`)**: the files are NOT in the npm package (`scripts/check-package.ts` fails if one enters it): they are served from the demo site, https://johnmorrisdotca.github.io/hoshi-stars/pictures/. The pictures are survey images that NASA's SkyView (https://skyview.gsfc.nasa.gov, a NASA service that cuts any survey into any frame on request) cut for us about each object in the tangent projection, north up and east left, on 2026-11-09. So where a picture lies on the sky is its request (centre, field and pixel count, below), not something fitted by eye. They are survey data made visible (a stretched composite of three bands as red, green and blue, with the sky made clear so that a picture over the chart adds light and no rectangle), not photographs a person took, and the card says so. Each survey's terms are those SkyView's page for it states, read on 2026-11-09:

Unless otherwise stated, images should be credited to the Sloan Digital Sky Survey. We provide all images on a Creative Commons Attribution license (CC-BY)." The credit is therefore given on every SDSS picture's card and in the table below, and the SDSS site (www.sdss.org) is linked here as the policy asks as a courtesy. The imaging is SDSS Data Release 9 as SkyView serves it.
  - **2MASS J, H and K** (https://skyview.gsfc.nasa.gov/current/cgi/moreinfo.pl?survey=2MASS-J): the Two Micron All Sky Survey, a US government work (see the 2MASS entry above). The acknowledgement the survey asks for: "This publication makes use of data products from the Two Micron All Sky Survey, which is a joint project of the University of Massachusetts and the Infrared Processing and Analysis Center/California Institute of Technology, funded by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration and the National Science Foundation."

(SkyView's page spells it "statment".)
  - **How a picture is made**: SkyView's runquery service, one request a band, `Projection=Tan`, `Coordinates=J2000`, `Sampler=Clip` (flux-conserving), `Return=FITS`, centred on the object's position in this package, a square field of 1.7 times the object's major diameter plus 3 arc-minutes (at least 8, at most 150; 62 for SDSS, which SkyView cannot cut wider in the minute it allows), at most 720 pixels. The bands go in as blue, green and red (the longest wavelength red), each with its black point at a low percentile of its own pixels and its white point at a high one, then a hyperbolic sine stretch that keeps the colour; the brightness is the picture's transparency. The script is `scripts/make-deep-sky-pictures.py`; the address of the first band of each picture is in the table below (the other two differ only in `Survey`).
  - **ESA/Hubble images (the finer pictures, 18, `pictures/<object>-<release>.webp`)**: each is the `screen` JPEG of an image of ESA/Hubble's (https://esahubble.org/images/<release>/, files at https://cdn.esahubble.org/archives/images/screen/<release>.jpg), read on 2026-11-09. ESA/Hubble's copyright page (https://esahubble.org/copyright/, read 2026-11-09) says: "ESA/Hubble images, videos and web texts are released under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International license and may on a non-exclusive basis be reproduced without fee provided they are clearly and visibly credited", and that the credit line "must be presented in a clear and readable manner to all users, with the wording unaltered"; each image's own credit line (its page's, and the `photoshop:Credit` tag in the file) is kept with it on the card and in the table below, and "if images or visuals are changed significantly from the original work (apart from resizing, cropping), we suggest that the changes are mentioned": ours are resized to at most 768 pixels, with the black of the sky and of the image's edges made transparent and no change of colour, and the card says so. The page says the licence does not extend to ESA/Hubble's logos (none is used) nor to its music, papers or code (none is used). A picture is placed on the sky from its own Astronomy Visualization Metadata (AVM tags in the file: the sky position of the reference pixel, which must be the middle of the image, the scale in degrees a pixel, the rotation, and a tangent projection, all required); an image whose reference pixel is not the middle is not used. These are NASA and ESA data, not photographs for this page: the releases' own credit lines name who made them.
  - **What is not used**: the Digitized Sky Survey (SkyView lists "STScI, ROE, AAO, UK-PPARC, CalTech, National Geographic Society" as its copyright holders, and its terms are not free), Stellarium's textures (GPL), the Wikimedia Commons and Wikipedia pictures of the Messier objects (each file has its own licence, and none was checked or used), HubbleSite and NASA's own copies of Hubble and Webb pictures (none was needed: ESA/Hubble's are the same data with an explicit licence), and ESO's and ESA/Webb's pictures (none is in the package yet, so there is no credit line for one). ## A constellation in 3D

`/constellation-3d` adds no data. Its positions are the bright-star table's right ascension, declination and parallax; a distance is 3,261.56 divided by the parallax in milliarcseconds, and its range is one standard error of the parallax each way (the error is from the Yale General Catalogue of Trigonometric Stellar Parallaxes, as above). A star with no parallax is given no distance. The quality classes (error under 5%, 5% to 20%, over 20%) and the viewpoint maths are this package's own. No figure from Hipparcos, Gaia or any other catalogue that cannot be passed on is in the package or its tests.

## Stellar profiles

The size, temperature and look of each star (`/profile`, `/portrait`) are worked out from the catalogue's V magnitude, B-V, spectral type and parallax by published calibrations. Every source below is a published table, fit or measurement read on 2026-11-09. The sentence relied on is the same for all of them: a measured or calibrated number is a fact, and facts are free to use; no paper's prose, figure or table layout is copied, only the numbers, which `scripts/make-profile-data.py` writes into `src/data/profile-tables.ts` (or which are typed as constants in `src/profile.ts`, with the table named beside them). None of them carries a licence that the package could not pass on, and none is ESA's Hipparcos, Tycho or Gaia data.

- **Temperature of dwarfs and subgiants** by spectral type and by B-V: E. Mamajek, "A Modern Mean Dwarf Stellar Color and Effective Temperature Sequence", version 2022.04.16 (https://www.pas.rochester.edu/~emamajek/EEM_dwarf_UBVIJHK_colors_Teff.txt), the table behind Table 5 of M. J. Pecaut and E. E. Mamajek (2013), ApJS 208, 9. The page asks that the paper be cited, which this line does; it states no licence. Columns read: type, Teff, BC_V, radius, B-V (types O3V to M9.5V).
- **Temperature and radius of giants** (G0III to M8III): the linear fits of G. T. van Belle, K. von Braun, D. R. Ciardi et al. (2021), "Direct Measurements of Giant Star Effective Temperatures and Linear Radii", ApJ 922, 163 (arXiv:2107.09205), Table 8 (temperature against spectral type) and Table 14 (radius against spectral type).
- **Temperature and bolometric correction of red supergiants** (K1.5 to M5): E. M. Levesque, P. Massey, K. A. G. Olsen et al. (2005), "The Effective Temperature Scale of Galactic Red Supergiants: Cool, But Not As Cool As We Thought", ApJ 628, 973 (arXiv:astro-ph/0504337), Table 5; the MARCS bolometric corrections of cool stars, Table 6.
- **Bolometric correction** of the other stars: the polynomial in log Teff of J. R. Flower (1996), ApJ 469, 355, with the coefficients G. Torres (2010) prints correctly in Table 1 of "On the Use of Empirical Bolometric Corrections for Stars", AJ 140, 1158 (arXiv:1008.3913). Mbol of the Sun 4.74 and the IAU 2015 nominal solar values (Resolutions B2 and B3).
- **Limb darkening**: A. Claret and S. Bloemen (2011), "Gravity and limb-darkening coefficients for the Kepler, CoRoT, Spitzer, uvby, UBVRIJHK, and Sloan photometric systems", A&A 529, A75; the linear coefficient u in the Johnson V band for ATLAS models at solar metallicity and 2 km/s microturbulence, from VizieR catalogue J/A+A/529/A75 (the Harvard mirror, https://vizier.cfa.harvard.edu/). This research used the VizieR catalogue access tool, CDS, Strasbourg (Ochsenbein, Bauer and Marcout 2000, A&AS 143, 23). A hot star has no low-gravity models; the nearest gravity the grid has at that temperature is used.
- **Measured shapes**: Altair, J. D. Monnier et al. (2007), "Imaging the Surface of Altair", Science 317, 342 (arXiv:0706.0867), Table 1, the model with the gravity-darkening coefficient free. Regulus, H. A. McAlister et al. (2005), ApJ 628, 439 (arXiv:astro-ph/0501261), Table 5. Vega, J. D. Monnier, X. Che, M. Zhao et al. (2012), ApJ 761, L3 (arXiv:1211.6055), Table 2, the "concordance" model.
- **Rotation speeds**: the projected rotation speed (v sin i) of 2,799 of the 5,080 stars, with its upper-limit (`<`) and uncertain (`:`) marks, is the Bright Star Catalogue's own `rotvel` column from the same HEASARC file credited under Bright stars above (same terms), written out by `scripts/make-rotation-data.py` into `src/data/rotation.ts` (read 2026-11-10). The rotation periods shown beside the measured shapes are those papers' own.
- **The checks** (in `test/`, not in the npm package) use published diameters: T. S. Boyajian et al. (2012), "Stellar Diameters and Temperatures I", ApJ 746, 101 (arXiv:1112.3316), Tables 1, 5 and 10 (43 stars; the paper's parallaxes are Hipparcos values, CC BY-NC, so they are not copied); P. Kervella et al. (2003), A&A 408, 681 (Sirius); S. Lacour et al. (2008), A&A 485, 561 (Arcturus); A. Richichi et al. (2016), arXiv:1609.06007 (Aldebaran); van Belle et al. (2021), Table 18 (Pollux); M. Montargès et al. (2013), EAS Publications Series 60, 167 (Betelgeuse); K. Ohnaka et al. (2013), arXiv:1304.4800 (Antares).
- **Not copied**: Wikipedia text, ESO and NASA photographs of resolved stars (the portrait is our own drawing from numbers, never a photograph), and any artist's impression. The surface texture is generated by a fixed pseudo-random sequence and is labelled as generic in every picture.

## The data door

- **The package's own credits (`OWN_SOURCES` in `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/door`, MODULES-831, written 2026-11-10)**: three rows, written from the entries of this file and read nowhere else. `yale-bsc` is the Yale Bright Star Catalogue (licence name "Public domain", the informal footing stated under The stars, read 2026-11-08). `ucac4` is UCAC4 (licence name "UCAC4 terms of use": free of charge with the release paper cited, as quoted under The stars, read 2026-11-09). `wikidata-deep-sky` is the Messier, NGC and IC list from Wikidata (CC0 1.0, read 2026-11-08). A test holds that none of the three is marked non-commercial or share-alike.
- **Data a caller hands in is not part of this package.** A source passed to `starSky` or `withObjects` carries its own credit and licence, and the credit line prints them (with "non-commercial" or "share-alike" where the source says so). This package bundles no such data and takes none as a dependency: the exclusions under The stars still stand.

## Deliberately not used

- ESA's Hipparcos (the original and the new reduction, van Leeuwen 2007, I/311), Tycho and Gaia catalogues: CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO (non-commercial), which an MIT package cannot pass on. Read again 2026-11-09 for the parallaxes: the terms are the same.
- HYG, AT-HYG and OpenNGC: CC BY-SA 4.0, which would put share-alike terms on this package's data. The NGC and IC objects come from Wikidata (CC0) instead, and none of OpenNGC's data is used.
- Stellarium's sky-culture files: GPL.
- d3-celestial's data files: the code is BSD-3-Clause but the data licence is not stated.
