## The stars

- **Bright stars (`@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars`)**: the Yale Bright Star Catalogue, D. Hoffleit and W. H. Warren Jr. (1991), "The Bright Star Catalogue, 5th Revised Edition (Preliminary Version)", Astronomical Data Center (NASA Goddard Space Flight Center) and the Centre de Données astronomiques de Strasbourg, catalogue V/50, as NASA's HEASARC serves it (table bsc5p, https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/W3Browse/star-catalog/bsc5p.html, file heasarc_bsc5p.tdat.gz, read 2026-11-08; a US government source), the 5,080 stars to visual magnitude 6.0, written out as numbers by `scripts/make-star-data.py` into `src/data/stars.ts`. The catalogue's own documentation calls it public domain and the Astronomical Data Center asks that it and the authors be acknowledged, which this line does; there is no formal licence text, so this footing is informal. **Confirmation requested 2026-11-09:** John Morris has written to NASA's HEASARC to confirm these terms. Until the answer comes, nothing is removed; the stars are the package's core, so no fallback is built.
  - **Spectral types and parallaxes of the bright stars**: the spectral type of each star is the Bright Star Catalogue's own string, whole (`M1-2Ia-Iab`). Its parallax is, where the Yale General Catalogue of Trigonometric Stellar Parallaxes (the source credited under Nearby stars below, same file, same terms) holds the star and gives a positive parallax, that catalogue's weighted mean parallax and standard error (matched by Henry Draper number, with the V magnitudes agreeing to 1.0; the components of a pair take the pair's parallax): 2,329 stars. Otherwise it is the Bright Star Catalogue's parallax to a whole milliarcsecond with no error (71 stars), or the catalogue's dynamical parallax (code D, 163 stars), which is kept in preference to a trigonometric one. Counts as of 2026-11-09, by `scripts/make-star-data.py`.
  - **Hipparcos new reduction, not used (read 2026-11-09)**: van Leeuwen (2007), VizieR catalogue I/311, would give better parallaxes but is ESA's. ESA's Hipparcos page (https://www.cosmos.esa.int/web/hipparcos/catalogues) says: "The Hipparcos and Tycho Catalogues are distributed under the CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO licence", with the credit "Credit: ESA". Non-commercial terms cannot be passed on under MIT, ESA's page for the new reduction states no other terms, and CDS's I/311 ReadMe could not be read (the host refused the connection), so the new reduction is treated as under the same non-commercial licence and none of its values, nor its HIP numbers, are read.
- **Nearby stars (`@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/nearby`)**: the stars that the Yale General Catalogue of Trigonometric Stellar Parallaxes holds and the bright stars do not, written out as numbers by `scripts/make-nearby-data.py` into `src/data/nearby.ts`. The build found 8,054 stars with a parallax in the catalogue (8,268 records, components and unresolved sums share a number); 2,489 are among the 5,080 bright stars (2,482 matched by HD number, 7 by position within 15 arc-seconds and magnitude within 1.0; 8 of the 2,489 are worth a second look) and **5,565 are new and written out**. Of the new: 4,352 have a 2MASS position and 1,213 keep the catalogue position (flagged coarse); 1,029 are flagged ambiguous; 496 have a parallax of 0 or less (excluded from 3D). Counts as of 2026-11-08.
  - **Yale GCTP**: W. F. van Altena, J. T. Lee and E. D. Hoffleit (1995), "The General Catalogue of Trigonometric Stellar Parallaxes, Fourth Edition", Yale University Observatory, catalogue I/238A of the Centre de Données astronomiques de Strasbourg (CDS) VizieR service, file `picat.dat` (1,173,412 bytes, SHA-256 `3b483e9ddf76e2fa9860adf2d25aae79ee257de428284c3351e5e81e402727a6`) from VizieR's Harvard mirror (https://vizier.cfa.harvard.edu/ftp/cats/I/238A/, read 2026-11-08). **No licence is stated**: the ReadMe has no licence, copyright or terms sentence, and VizieR's own page says commercial use of its data "is subject to rules depending of the origin". By this package's rule that data with no licence is used with credit, it is used here with the credit above, as measured numbers (parallaxes, positions, magnitudes), not the printed book or its text. **It is removed on request**: write to the repository's owner and the stars come out of the next release. The positions are B1900.0 in the FK4 system; they are carried to J2000 ICRS with astropy's FK4 to FK5 transformation after the catalogue's own proper motion has moved them. The identifiers `GJ` (Gliese-Jahreiss) and `HD` are numbers only; no data of those catalogues is read. **Confirmation requested 2026-11-09:** John Morris has written to the CDS to confirm these terms. Until the answer comes, nothing is removed; a build without it (`/nearby` left out, the bright stars back on the Bright Star Catalogue's own parallaxes) is being made ready (MODULES-779).
  - **2MASS**: the modern positions are from the Two Micron All Sky Survey Point Source Catalog (Cutri et al. 2003), read on 2026-11-08 through NASA/IPAC's Infrared Science Archive TAP service (https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/TAP, table `fp_psc`), in batches by `scripts/make-nearby-data.py`; the designation and the position error of each source are kept. IRSA lists it as a US government work (https://www.usa.gov/government-works) and IPAC asks for this acknowledgement, which this line gives: "This publication makes use of data products from the Two Micron All Sky Survey, which is a joint project of the University of Massachusetts and the Infrared Processing and Analysis Center/California Institute of Technology, funded by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration and the National Science Foundation."
  - **Not used**: Gaia, Hipparcos and Tycho values (CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO). Their catalogue numbers are not read either.
- **Faint stars (`@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/faint`, the files in `faint-tiles/`)**: the fourth U.S. Naval Observatory CCD Astrograph Catalog, UCAC4: N. Zacharias, C. T. Finch, T. M. Girard, A. Henden, J. L. Bartlett, D. G. Monet and M. I. Zacharias (2013), "The Fourth US Naval Observatory CCD Astrograph Catalog (UCAC4)", Astronomical Journal 145, 44 (VizieR catalogue I/322A), read on 2026-11-09 through NASA/IPAC's Infrared Science Archive TAP service (https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/TAP, table `ucac4_sources`, dataset DOI 10.26131/IRSA17) in 18 declination bands, by `scripts/make-faint-data.py`, which writes 2,462,539 stars to magnitude 12 into 3,338 sky tiles. The raw downloads are kept outside the repository. The sentences relied on, all read on 2026-11-09:
  - **Terms of use.** UCAC4 states no licence. The catalogue's own documentation (`ucac4doc.txt`, USNO's "readme_u4", from the VizieR mirror at https://vizier.cfa.harvard.edu/ftp/cats/I/322A/) says: "The UCAC4 comes free of charge." and "The UCAC4 release paper should be cited whenever UCAC data are utilized."; its VizieR ReadMe says: "The paper (Zacharias et al., 2013, AJ, 145, 44) should be cited whenever UCAC4 data is utilized." IRSA's overview of the catalogue (https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/data/USNO/UCAC4/overview.html) says: "If you use UCAC4, please cite both Zacharias et al. (2013) and the dataset Digital Object Identifier (DOI)". So the footing is free use with credit, and this line is the credit: please cite Zacharias et al. (2013) and DOI 10.26131/IRSA17. USNO's own catalogue page (https://www.usno.navy.mil/USNO/astrometry/optical-IR-prod/ucac) answers a script with "Access Denied" (HTTP 403) and CDS's licence page (https://cds.unistra.fr/vizier-org/licences_vizier.html) could not be reached (the host refused the connection), so neither was read; the work is by US Naval Observatory staff. IRSA asks: "This research has made use of the NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive, which is funded by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration and operated by the California Institute of Technology." (https://irsa.ipac.caltech.edu/ack.html), which this line also does.
  - **APASS.** UCAC4 carries the AAVSO Photometric All-Sky Survey's V and B magnitudes (DR6). The magnitude of a faint star and its colour are APASS's, and VizieR's ReadMe for APASS (II/336, https://vizier.cfa.harvard.edu/ftp/cats/II/336/ReadMe) asks: "This publication makes use of data products from the AAVSO Photometric All Sky Survey (APASS). Funded by the Robert Martin Ayers Sciences Fund and the National Science Foundation." The AAVSO's own page (https://www.aavso.org/apass) answers a script with HTTP 403 and was not read.
  - **What is read.** Position at J2000.0 (`ra`, `dec`), APASS V (`vmag`) and B (`bmag`), UCAC's own model magnitude (`momag`, 579 to 642 nm, between V and R), and the flags `ojt` (object type), `tf` (the FK6-Hipparcos-Tycho source flag), `ver` and `ber` (the V and B errors), `dsf`. No proper motion, parallax, 2MASS or other value is read. The magnitude is APASS V where the record has one that is APASS's own, else UCAC's model magnitude (of the rows not dropped as object type 4, 6 or 7, 4,308,852 had the first, 401,001 the second); it is stored to 0.05. The colour is APASS B-V where both are APASS's own, else none (222,142 of the 2,462,539 stars have none).
  - **Not Hipparcos, Tycho or Gaia.** UCAC4 copies in values from the Hipparcos and Tycho-2 catalogues (ESA's, CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO, see above) and from the FK6, "for all bright stars not observed with the astrograph" and where its own measure was poor ("the FK6/Hipparcos/Tycho-2 astrometric data are used instead of the UCAC observational data", `ucac4doc.txt`). Every such row is dropped, by UCAC4's own flags: object type 6 (position and proper motion substituted from FK6, Hipparcos or Tycho-2; 34,472 rows), object type 7 (a supplement star from them with no CCD data; 4,215 rows) and object type 4 (external high-proper-motion data used instead of UCAC's own, whose source the catalogue does not name; 4,932 rows). Where the source flag `tf` is not 0 and the APASS error is 0, the V (or B) in the record is Tycho's Vt (or Bt), by the catalogue's own words ("apasm(2) < 20000 and apase(2) = 0"), and is not used: 72,164 stars were measured by UCAC's model magnitude instead. A record whose `tf` is not 0 but which is none of the above has UCAC's own position and magnitude and a cross-reference to Tycho-2 only; John chose on 2026-11-09 to keep them (2,027,855 of the 2,462,539 stars), and the guide says so. The weighted-mean positions are on the ICRS "as represented by the Hipparcos / Tycho-2 catalogs", which is the reference frame and no value.
  - **Not the bright stars twice.** 45 rows within 20 arc-seconds of one of the 5,080 bright stars and within 1.5 magnitudes of it are dropped. Of 4,753,472 rows read, 2,247,269 were fainter than 12.0 (the download takes a margin of half a magnitude), and 43,619 were dropped as above.
  - **What the layer is not.** It is thin brighter than about magnitude 9, because the stars UCAC4 only copied are the bright ones (3,138 stars between 6.5 and 8, where the sky holds far more): the Bright Star Catalogue to 6.0 and this layer do not make a complete catalogue between 6 and 9. The guide shows the counts.
- **Star names**: the IAU Working Group on Star Names (WGSN, https://www.iau.org/science/scientific_bodies/working_groups/280/), "IAU Catalog of Star Names (IAU-CSN)" (https://www.pas.rochester.edu/~emamajek/WGSN/IAU-CSN.txt, "Last updated 2022-04-04", read 2026-11-08). The names are facts (which star the IAU named what), typed as a table keyed by Bright Star Catalogue (HR) number in `scripts/make-star-data.py`: every name in the list whose star is among the 5,080 (332 names), matched by the list's HR designation, and spelled as its ASCII name column spells it. The list states that IAU-produced products are released under Creative Commons Attribution, "as long as the source is mentioned", which this line does; only the names are used, none of its text. Three more names are traditional ones that are not on the IAU list: Navi (γ Cas, HR 264), Men (α Lup, HR 5469) and Rutilicus (ζ Her, HR 6212); 335 names in all.
- **Star names in Japanese (`Star.nameJa`, `src/data/star-names-ja.ts`)**: the katakana spelling of each of the 335 names, written by hand for this package. A transliteration of a name is not a creative work, so no licence is needed, and no list is copied: no Wikipedia text and no source with non-commercial or share-alike terms. The spellings follow the usual Japanese forms; those of the 27 stars on the National Astronomical Observatory of Japan's "おもな恒星の名前" page (https://eco.mtk.nao.ac.jp/koyomi/faq/stars.html, read 2026-11-08, copyright NAOJ) were compared with it. That page lists 27 stars (Acrux among them); 26 of the 27 spellings were already ours and agreed, except that it has アダーラ for Adhara where we had アダラ, now アダーラ (the comparison only; that page is not copied). The other 308 are our own reading of the IAU spelling and **wait for a Japanese reviewer**; a table of them for the reviewer is kept outside the repository.
- **Star colour**: Ballesteros's B-V to temperature relation (F. J. Ballesteros 2012, EPL 97, 34008) and a piecewise black-body colour fit.

- **Precession to B1875.0**: the rigorous angles of the IAU 1976 system as J. Meeus gives them in "Astronomical Algorithms" (2nd ed., 1998), chapter 21, written out as formulas; no code is copied. The test compares it with Herget's precession as Roman's own program applies it.

## The constellation figures

- **Figures (`@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/lines`)**: our own. For each of the 88 constellations the stars to join were chosen for this package from the positions and brightness of the Yale stars and written as lists of Bright Star Catalogue (HR) numbers in `src/data/lines.ts`. No figure is copied or transcribed from a book, a star atlas, Sky & Telescope, H. A. Rey, the IAU's charts, Stellarium or d3-celestial; the common shapes (the Dipper, Orion's belt, the W) are the plain joins of bright stars that anyone would draw. Every star of a line lies inside the constellation (checked with the boundaries in `test/lines.test.ts`).

## Sky cultures

- **Sky cultures (`@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/culture`, `.../cultures/western`, `.../cultures/japanese`, `.../cultures/chinese`)**: which stars make each figure is a fact; the data are facts typed or read from the sources below, and the lines are our own joins. No file of Stellarium's sky cultures (GPL, CC BY-SA), no Wikipedia text (CC BY-SA) and no source with non-commercial or share-alike terms is read. Read 2026-11-09 unless said otherwise.
  - **The traditional Chinese sky (`src/data/culture-chinese.ts`, by `scripts/make-culture-data.py`)**: Wikidata (https://www.wikidata.org), CC0 1.0 (https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing: "All structured data (i.e. the main, Property, Lexeme, and EntitySchema namespaces) is released into the public domain under Creative Commons Zero."), through its SPARQL endpoint https://query.wikidata.org/sparql. The query is the script's `QUERY`: every item with a "catalog code" (P528) that has the Bright Star Catalogue (Q499138) as its catalogue ("HR 5056"), with its Chinese label (`zh`) and aliases. A name that ends in a Chinese number is star N of the asterism before it (`角宿一` is the first star of `角宿`), and 3,361 of the 8,974 stars with an HR number carried a Chinese name. The script keeps the names that belong to the Three Enclosures and Twenty-Eight Mansions (238 asterisms; 195 have two stars or more); the asterism names with their Traditional spelling and pinyin are the script's own `NAMES` table (typed for this package, the pinyin written by hand and checked for the characters that read two ways such as 宿 xiù, 参 shēn, 螣 téng, 更 gēng and 騎 jì), and the proper-named stars of the Northern Dipper, Sirius, Canopus and Arcturus are the script's `SUPPLEMENT`. The English names and the quarter of the sky (Azure Dragon, Black Tortoise, White Tiger, Vermilion Bird) of the 28 mansions are Wikidata's English labels and its "part of" statements. Wikidata is crowd-edited and some numbers in it are wrong (the Dipper mansion 斗宿 numbers its stars 1, 1, 2, 4, 5, 6): the stars of an asterism are taken from the names, not the numbers, and the count of stars we lack (`missing`) is only a lower limit. Two asterisms whose names hold a character that most phones cannot draw (`近𫓧𫓰`, `𫓧锧`) and one with a stray name (`钩钤一和钩钤`) are left out. A name found on stars far apart (`柱`, `天田`, `杵`, `五诸侯` are each the name of two asterisms in the old lists) is split into groups within 30 degrees of each other.
  - **The Chinese names of the 88 constellations, Traditional (`src/data/constellation-names-zh.ts`)**: the National Academy for Educational Research (Taiwan, 國家教育研究院), 天文學學術名詞 (astronomy terms, English with Chinese), as the dataset 「國家教育研究院-天文學學術名詞」 of the Taiwan government's open data (https://scidm.nchc.org.tw/en/dataset/best_wish15166, file 國家教育研究院-天文學學術名詞.csv, 6,091 terms, read 2026-11-09; the dataset page says 「政府資料開放授權條款-第1版」, https://data.gov.tw/license). That licence (Open Government Data License, version 1.0) lets anyone use the data for any purpose, commercial included, free of royalty, and pass it on, and it asks for the provider to be named, which this line does: 國家教育研究院 (National Academy for Educational Research), 天文學學術名詞. The list writes each name with an optional 星 (`仙女[星]座`); the short form `仙女座` is written here.
  - **The Chinese names of the 88 constellations, Simplified**: the Traditional names written in the mainland's characters by a conversion table, with 蝘蜓 for 蝘蜒, 后发 for 后髮 and 宝瓶 for the mainland's astronomical name of Aquarius (not 水瓶, the name from astrology). 79 of the 86 constellations Wikidata (CC0) has a Chinese label for agree with it; of the other 7, six are labels Wikidata holds only in Traditional characters and one (Aquarius) is 水瓶座. ## The constellation pictures and facts

- **Pictures (`@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/thumbnails`, and the files in `thumbnails/`)**: our own drawings, made from this package's stars, figures and boundaries by `src/thumbnails.ts`; nothing is traced or copied. The PNGs are drawn from the SVGs by resvg (MPL-2.0), a development tool: none of its code is in the package.
- **Facts (`@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/constellation-facts`, `src/data/constellation-facts.ts`)**: facts typed by hand in our own words, no text copied. The hemisphere, the best month and how easy a constellation is to find are worked out from this package's own data. Who made each constellation and when (Ptolemy's Almagest, about AD 150; Keyser and de Houtman, 1595 to 1597; Plancius; Vopel; Hevelius, 1687; Lacaille, 1756) is as the standard histories of the constellations give it, and the count of 50 of today's 88 from Ptolemy (47 and the three parts of Argo Navis) agrees with the IAU's constellation pages. The meteor showers are the established showers of the IAU Meteor Data Center, with the usual peak nights of the International Meteor Organization's yearly calendar; these were typed on 2026-11-09 without re-reading either page that day, and a peak can be a day or two off in any year. The asterism names are the common ones. The Japanese words are our own, short and everyday, and await a native reader. No Wikipedia text is used.
- **NOIRLab's constellation and all-sky photos are not used.** NOIRLab's terms (https://noirlab.edu/public/copyright/, read 2026-11-09) say "Unless specifically noted, the images, and videos distributed on the public NOIRLab website ... are licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License", with credit, but each picture names its own credit and the constellation charts there are another group's artwork, so a photo style needs a picture-by-picture check; it is not built.

## The map projections

- **Projections (`@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/projection`)**: stereographic, orthographic, gnomonic, azimuthal equidistant, equirectangular, Mollweide and Hammer, written from the formulas in J. P. Snyder, "Map Projections: A Working Manual", U.S. Geological Survey Professional Paper 1395 (1987), a U.S. Government work in the public domain. The formulas are mathematics; no library's code is copied. **Confirmation requested 2026-11-09:** John Morris has written to NASA's HEASARC to confirm these terms. Until the answer comes, nothing is removed; a Milky Way built from IRAS, COBE DIRBE and our own model only is being made ready (MODULES-780).

- **The whole-sky map (`@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/all-sky`)**: the same Mollweide, Hammer and plate carrée formulas (Snyder), fitted to a 2:1 box, with the IAU's galactic frame (below) as one of its two orientations. It adds no data: the demo's whole-sky page draws the boundaries, figures, stars and Milky Way brightness this package already carries, with the credits given for each of them in this file.

## The Milky Way

- **Milky Way brightness (`@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/milky-way`)**: a grid of 360 by 120 numbers of 0 to 31 (1 degree of galactic longitude by 0.5 degree of latitude, within 30 degrees of the plane) in `src/data/milky-way.ts`, written by `scripts/make-milky-way-data.py` from three sources. No image of the sky is read, copied or traced.
  - **Star counts: the SAO Star Catalog** (Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory Star Catalog, 258,944 stars to about visual magnitude 9.3; catalogue I/131A at the CDS), as NASA's HEASARC serves it (table `sao`, https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/W3Browse/star-catalog/sao.html, file https://heasarc.gsfc.nasa.gov/FTP/heasarc/dbase/tdat_files/heasarc_sao.tdat.gz, read 2026-11-08). **No licence is stated for this catalogue: it is used with credit to the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory and NASA's HEASARC, and removed on request.** Only the stars of visual magnitude 7.0 to 9.0 are counted, on an equal-area grid, and only the counts (a handful of smoothed numbers per square degree) are kept: no star of the catalogue is in the package. The counts show where the Milky Way's stars are thick and, where the catalogue falls short of its surroundings, where near dust (the Coalsack) hides the stars behind it.
  - **Dust: the IRAS Sky Survey Atlas, 100 micron** (NASA/IPAC Infrared Science Archive; IRAS was a NASA, Dutch and British mission), as NASA's SkyView serves it (https://skyview.gsfc.nasa.gov, survey `IRAS 100`, read 2026-11-08), which labels the survey "Copyright: Public Domain" and its provenance "NASA IPAC/Jet Propulsion Laboratory". Where the atlas has unobserved strips, **COBE DIRBE's zodi-subtracted mission-average 100 micron map** (the COBE Team, NASA Goddard; SkyView survey `COBE DIRBE/ZSMA`, labelled "Copyright: Public Domain") fills them, on a scale matched in the plane. The map's glow compared with the glow around it says where the dust lanes are (the Great Rift, the Cygnus Rift); it is not an image of them. "IRAS" in SkyView is the ISSA atlas, not the Schlegel, Finkbeiner and Davis map nor IRIS, which others derived from it and which are not used.
  - **The bulge and the disk: our own figures.** The bright core toward Sagittarius is a Gaussian some 8 degrees along the plane and 3.5 across, and the glow along the plane follows an exponential disk (scale length 2.6 kpc, the Sun 8.2 kpc from the centre) added up along each line of sight: round, published values of the Galaxy's size, not a copy of any map or model. The counts change the disk's glow only by how much each direction is richer or poorer than its neighbourhood.
  - **Galactic frame**: the IAU's (north galactic pole at right ascension 192.85948 and declination 27.12825 degrees, and the north celestial pole at galactic longitude 122.93192 degrees, J2000), written as formulas.
  - **Not used**: any photograph or drawing of the Milky Way (ESO's panorama, NASA's and ESA's pictures and Gaia's colour map were looked at, nowhere in the data or code, only to judge the result by eye); Planck's maps; the Schlegel, Finkbeiner and Davis dust map; Stellarium's textures (GPL) and anyone's artwork.

## Your sky

- **Horizon view (`@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/sky`)**: written from the formulas in J. Meeus, "Astronomical Algorithms", 2nd ed. (Willmann-Bell, 1998): Greenwich mean sidereal time (the IAU 1982 expression, chapter 12), equatorial to horizontal coordinates (chapter 13), atmospheric refraction (Saemundsson's formula, chapter 16) and precession from J2000 (the IAU 1976 constants, chapter 21). The formulas are mathematics and the worked examples (12.a, 12.b, 13.b, 21.b) are used only as test values; no book text or library code is copied.
- **UTC to Terrestrial Time (`horizontalFrom`)**: the leap seconds (TAI - UTC, 10 s in 1972 to 37 s in 2017, a table of facts published by the IERS and the BIPM) plus 32.184 s; before 1972, delta T by the polynomial fits of F. Espenak and J. Meeus ("Polynomial Expressions for Delta T", NASA Eclipse Web Site, 2006), written out as formulas in `src/time-scale.ts`; no code is copied. The test compares the leap-second part with wakusei-planets' conversion, a dev-only second opinion.
- **Another planet**: the convention is that of the IAU Working Group on Cartographic Coordinates and Rotational Elements (pole right ascension and declination, and W = W0 + rate d), as in Archinal et al., Celestial Mechanics and Dynamical Astronomy 130, 22 (2018). The tests type in the 2015 Mars values from that report; the package itself holds no planet data.

## A constellation in 3D

`/constellation-3d` adds no data. Its positions are the bright-star table's right ascension, declination and parallax; a distance is 3,261.56 divided by the parallax in milliarcseconds, and its range is one standard error of the parallax each way (the error is from the Yale General Catalogue of Trigonometric Stellar Parallaxes, as above). A star with no parallax is given no distance. The quality classes (error under 5%, 5% to 20%, over 20%) and the viewpoint maths are this package's own. No figure from Hipparcos, Gaia or any other catalogue that cannot be passed on is in the package or its tests.

## Stellar profiles

The size, temperature and look of each star (`/profile`, `/portrait`) are worked out from the catalogue's V magnitude, B-V, spectral type and parallax by published calibrations. Every source below is a published table, fit or measurement read on 2026-11-09. The sentence relied on is the same for all of them: a measured or calibrated number is a fact, and facts are free to use; no paper's prose, figure or table layout is copied, only the numbers, which `scripts/make-profile-data.py` writes into `src/data/profile-tables.ts` (or which are typed as constants in `src/profile.ts`, with the table named beside them). Bloemen (2011), "Gravity and limb-darkening coefficients for the Kepler, CoRoT, Spitzer, uvby, UBVRIJHK, and Sloan photometric systems", A&A 529, A75; the linear coefficient u in the Johnson V band for ATLAS models at solar metallicity and 2 km/s microturbulence, from VizieR catalogue J/A+A/529/A75 (the Harvard mirror, https://vizier.cfa.harvard.edu/). - **Not copied**: Wikipedia text, ESO and NASA photographs of resolved stars (the portrait is our own drawing from numbers, never a photograph), and any artist's impression. The surface texture is generated by a fixed pseudo-random sequence and is labelled as generic in every picture.

## Deliberately not used

- ESA's Hipparcos (the original and the new reduction, van Leeuwen 2007, I/311), Tycho and Gaia catalogues: CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO (non-commercial), which an MIT package cannot pass on. Read again 2026-11-09 for the parallaxes: the terms are the same.
- HYG, AT-HYG and OpenNGC: CC BY-SA 4.0, which would put share-alike terms on this package's data. The NGC and IC objects come from Wikidata (CC0) instead, and none of OpenNGC's data is used.
- Stellarium's sky-culture files: GPL.
- d3-celestial's data files: the code is BSD-3-Clause but the data licence is not stated.
