# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

- **How well each star is known** (MODULES-654, first slice). New entry point `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/accuracy`: `starAccuracy(star)` takes a bright star or a nearby star and returns its `grade` (A, B or C) and, for `position`, `magnitude`, `colour`, `parallax` and `properMotion`, the `source` (`BSC`, `GCTP`, `2MASS`, `dynamical` or `literature`), the `error` the source states (never a guessed one) and the `resolution` (the size of the last printed digit, a floor on the error), with a `note` where there is something to watch; plus `distance` (`relativeError`, `quality`). The grade is a rule on those figures (`GRADE_RULES`): A a distance known to under 5% and a modern position (2 arc-seconds or better), B a distance known to 20% or better, C anything else. `gradeAtLeast(grade, minimum)` and `STAR_GRADES` come with it. Of the 5,080 bright stars 68 are A, 461 B and 4,551 C; of the 5,565 nearby stars 235 are A, 1,282 B and 4,048 C (guide: "How well a star is known", English and Japanese). The data export's `stars` and `nearby-stars` tables gain `grade` and `distanceQuality` columns, and `stars` gains `positionSource` and `positionResolutionArcsec`. `distanceQuality`, `DISTANCE_QUALITIES` and `QUALITY_LIMITS` moved to a small file of their own so `/accuracy` loads no star table; `/constellation-3d` still exports them.
- **Keep only the stars you can trust, and say when two answers tie** (MODULES-654, second slice). `stars`, `nearbyStars` and `nearest` take `minGrade` (`"A"`, `"B"` or `"C"`: the worst grade to keep; anything else is a `RangeError` naming the option; a deep-sky object has no grade). `/accuracy` gains `starGrade(star)` (the grade alone, the same answer as `starAccuracy(star).grade`, cheap enough to filter thousands of stars) and `checkGrade`. A query by space gives each answer a `range` (the distance one `error` each way, never below 0) and `tied`, true where that range overlaps the one before or after it in the full list; an answer with no stated `error` has no range and is never tied. The guide says that a bright star's position grade uses the printed resolution, since the Bright Star Catalogue states no error.

## [0.39.0] - 2026-11-12

- **The demo's header links the Markers page** (MODULES-692): "Markers" in English, マーカー in Japanese, beside the other demo pages.
- **Supergiant distances are no longer printed too near** (MODULES-1180). The Yale parallax of Betelgeuse (9.8 +/- 4.7 mas) printed 333 light-years, and Rigel's 351; the usual modern figures are far larger. Betelgeuse now takes Joyce et al. 2020 (ApJ 902, 63): 168 (+27/-15) pc, 548 light-years, range 480 to 640. Rigel takes de Almeida et al. 2022 (arXiv:2204.00372, DOI 10.1093/mnras/stac1617): 0.26 kpc, 847 light-years, a range from 590 to 1,480 that spans the paper's two luminosity models. Polaris takes the Hubble parallax of Polaris B (Bond et al. 2018, ApJ 853, 55): 521 light-years, with the error widened so the range reaches 430 light-years (other measurements put it nearer, at about 430-450) and a note saying so. Antares, Canopus and Mu Cephei have no source that can be passed on under MIT, so their Yale parallax keeps its value with the error widened to the parallax itself: `rough`, no upper limit. New: `Star.parallaxSource` can be `"literature"`, and `Star.parallaxNote` names the paper or says the error was widened (6 stars; `null` in the exported table for the rest). Every distance derived from a star's parallax follows: `starDistance`, the distance layers, the constellation in 3D, the sky from another star and the profile's radius (Betelgeuse 553 Suns, range 317 to 987). Sources: `NOTICE.md`. Tests: the six stars against the chosen values, and that no other star carries a note.
- **Your own points on the chart, with a time slider** (MODULES-692). `<hoshi-chart>` and `chartSvg` take `markers` (`SkyMarker`: `ra`, `dec`, and `date`, `label`, `color`, `size`, `group`, `id`), `timeRange` (`{ from, to, fade }`: only the points dated inside it are drawn, a point with no date always is, and `fade` days bring a point in from an edge) and `tracks` (`true` or group names: each group joined by a line in date order). On the element they are properties or the attributes `markers` (JSON), `time-from`, `time-to`, `time-fade` and `tracks`; a click, a tap, or Enter or Space on a point that has the focus sends `hoshi-marker` with `{ marker, id, index, source }` and no `hoshi-pick`. Each point drawn is a button with its name (40 reached with Tab, the focus kept through a redraw). The names go through the chart's own label placer: placed before the stars' names, clear of the stars and of each other, and the stars' names keep off the dots. Nothing is drawn at rest and nothing leaves the page. The pure parts are exported from `/chart`: `visibleMarkers`, `markerTracks`, `markerSpan`, `markerTime`, `normaliseMarkers`, `markersToCsv` and `markersFromCsv`. A new page of the demo, `markers.html`, has a sample, a slider with two handles and Play, a card for the chosen point, and CSV and JSON in and out, with the reader's own dates. A new guide, "Your own points on the chart" (English and Japanese), and the usage checks of `examples/markers`. Tests: the range, the fade, the track order, the CSV round trip, the names kept clear of the stars and each other, the element's click, Enter and Space, and the demo page.
- **The Milky Way as a texture for a WebGL sky** (MODULES-631). `milkyWayTexture({ width, frame, detail, ramp })` from `/milky-way` paints the brightness map as a picture of the whole sky, twice as wide as high (2048 by 1024 by default, up to 4096 across), in right ascension and declination or in galactic coordinates, as red, green, blue and opacity ready for `ImageData` or a WebGL texture; `MILKY_WAY_NIGHT_RAMP` is its default colours. It is made from the package's own numbers, no photograph. `milkyWayBrightness(point, { detail })` reads the grid sharper (from 0 to 2; 0, the plain reading, stays the default there, 1 is the texture's): along smooth curves, each value pushed away from the mean round it, so the Great Rift, the Coalsack and the Cygnus Rift are narrower and darker and the star clouds beside them brighter, with no noise and nothing added. The demo's whole-sky map is painted with it.

- **The 3D freeze test counts the GPU objects at rest, not after a fixed wait** (MODULES-1068). "Changing the constellation, the viewpoint and the look over and over gives back what it took" waited a fixed 1.6 s after each round for the shared assets a view left to be let go (the cache keeps them a second), and on a busy Mac read 85 against at most 51. 85 is exactly one view's assets not yet let go: with the release made three seconds late on purpose, the old test read 85 and 123 round after round. Each round now waits for what the page reports, every shared asset held having a user again (the cache's entries equal to its references, one view live), and then for the GPU objects to stand still; with the late release it reads 47 every round. The allowance is unchanged; the test has three minutes for its 24 rounds. Geometries that are never freed (47 to 1,529) still fail it.

- **The faint-stars frame test holds the layer's work on CI** (MODULES-1053). "Panning the zoomed-in map: the faint stars add little to a frame" measured 83.5 ms against its bar of 81.5 (1.3 times the 55 ms without them, plus 10) once on a shared runner; in the CI image on one CPU the same drag read 128 to 133 ms with and 113 to 119 without, so a runner's clock says how busy it is. On CI the test now holds what does not vary: at every tenth step of the drag the faint layer is at most 18 paths (one for each size and colour class) holding at most `FAINT_DOT_CAP` dots, and it draws; the 250 ms ceiling stays. This computer keeps the time bar. A path for every star (797 paths, with frame times hardly changed) fails it on CI, and a faint layer 25 ms slower (54.6 against a bar of 46.9) fails it here.

- **The nearest-star timing test holds under load** (MODULES-1054). "One query over all 5,080 stars costs no more than four plain passes" failed on a Mac with a load average of 22: the fastest of five rounds, the query's and the yardstick's timed apart. It now first counts the work, which no load changes: a query by sky takes one angle for every other star (at a date too), and by space one distance for each star with a parallax and at most one more for its error; a second pass fails it. The time is then taken in turns, the yardstick and the query one after the other, the fastest of fifteen rounds each. This computer holds the budget of four; CI holds the work and a ceiling of twenty. Under 16 busy processes (load 16.5) the old test failed 2 of 8 runs and the new one none; a slow query (6.0 plain passes) still fails it.

## [0.6.0] - 2026-11-08

- New entry point `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/nearby`: `nearbyStars(options)` and `nearbyStarByName(text)` give 5,565 more stars from the Yale General Catalogue of Trigonometric Stellar Parallaxes (Proxima Centauri, Barnard's Star, Wolf 359 and the red dwarfs), none of them among the 5,080 bright stars, each with names and identifiers, a J2000 position with its source and error (4,352 from 2MASS, 1,213 the Yale catalogue's own and flagged coarse), parallax and distance with their errors, proper motion, V magnitude, spectral type and flags. About 240 kB gzipped, its own import; the bright-star import is unchanged. `nearest(..., { by: "space", among: [...stars(), ...nearbyStars()] })` counts them, and works out `error` for a nearby star. `scripts/make-nearby-data.py` builds the table (the Yale file is checked against a SHA-256, 2MASS is read from IRSA in batches). NOTICE.md carries the credits: the Yale catalogue states no licence and is removed on request; 2MASS's acknowledgement.

## [0.5.0] - 2026-11-08

- New entry point `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/nearest`: `nearest(target, { count, within, kind, by, date, magnitude, among })` and `within(target, radius, options)` give the stars or Messier objects nearest a star, an object, a name or a point, nearest first, by angle on the sky (`{ thing, angle }`) or by distance in space from the parallaxes (`{ thing, distance, parsecs, error }`, with a `skipped` count; a point `{ x, y, z }` in parsecs is a target too), and `neighbouringConstellations(text)` gives the constellations that share a piece of boundary. One query over all 5,080 stars takes about 1 ms. The space answers cover only the bright-star catalogue until a nearby-star catalogue lands.
- The demo has a Nearby panel: pick a star, a Messier object or a constellation and it lists the ten nearest stars (in the sky, or in space for a star with a parallax) or the constellations that touch it, and rings them on the chart; `by=space` is kept in the address. The embed page does the same from `near=S:2491` (or `D:M31`, `C:Ori`) and `by=space`, and the code to copy carries them.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-11-08

- The demo's chart and the embed page take the family's hands (kazoku-ui's view controller): a drag turns the sky, two fingers pinch, Ctrl or Command and the wheel zoom about the pointer, `+`, `-`, the arrow keys and `0` work with the chart focused, and the double tap zooms. At the opening view the plain wheel scrolls the page, and a finger's up-and-down drag does too; zoomed in, the wheel and a drag move the sky. The embed page (`embed.html`) is no longer a still picture: it has the same hands and zoom in, zoom out and back-to-start buttons, and writes `ra`, `dec` and `zoom` into its own address.
- The zoom now runs from 0.1 (the whole sky, small) to 500 (about 0.7 degree across a desk-wide chart, where Mizar and Alcor still draw as two stars), up from 0.6 to 60. Lines and the Milky Way keep drawing at the top of the range: a point is placed out to 100,000 pixels from the middle (it was six times the chart's size), so a segment that crosses the chart is not lost. Constellation names are left out below 0.25, where they cannot be told apart.
- New npm keywords `react`, `galaxy` and `component`.

- The family's thumbnail, dark thumbnail and icon (`docs/images`, made by `pnpm images`): a sky chart of Orion with its lines on the Milky Way. The README opens with it and the demo carries it as its link card (og:image) and apple-touch-icon.
- `pnpm test:readme` runs every `js` and `ts` example of the README and the guides against the built package, and checks that they name every export and every picture exists. Run by CI and by the release.
- The demo keeps less empty room under its title on a phone. Fixed: the demo's chart failed to draw (an error in the console) once zoomed in past 5, with the Milky Way on. The getting-started guide said Orion has 12 lines to draw; it has 8.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-11-08

- 199 more common star names, 335 in all: every name on the IAU Catalog of Star Names whose star is in the 5,080, among them Acrux, Alcor, the Pleiades (Alcyone, Merope, Electra, Atlas, Maia, Taygeta, Pleione, Celaeno, Asterope), Rasalas, Mesarthim, Zosma and Tiaki. Each has its katakana (`アクルックス`, `アルコル`, `アルキオネ`), and the demo's search finds them in both. Adhara's katakana is now `アダーラ`, as the National Astronomical Observatory of Japan writes it. The source and its date are in `NOTICE.md`.

## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-08

- New entry point `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/milky-way`: a brightness map of the Milky Way (about 11 kB of numbers) with the bright core toward Sagittarius and the Great Rift, the Coalsack and the Cygnus Rift as dimmer places. `milkyWayBrightness(point)` gives 0 to 1 toward a point of the sky, `milkyWayBands(levels, { step })` cuts it into ribbons of sky points to fill as soft bands, and `toGalactic` and `fromGalactic` change between right ascension and declination and galactic longitude and latitude. Made by `scripts/make-milky-way-data.py` from SAO star counts (NASA HEASARC; no licence stated, used with credit and removed on request), the IRAS 100 micron and COBE DIRBE dust maps (NASA, public domain) and a bulge of our own figures; see `NOTICE.md`.
- The demo: a **Milky Way** (天の川) button draws the band under the stars and the lines, in every projection, on by default; `layers=milky` is in the address and in the embed page's.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-11-08

- New `Star.nameJa`: the katakana name (`シリウス`, `ベテルギウス`) of each of the 136 named stars, which `starByName` also finds. The spellings are the IAU names written in Japanese letters by hand, with the usual Japanese forms; see `NOTICE.md`.
- The demo: a star's name or a constellation's is left out where it would sit on another label, bright stars first, and comes back when you zoom in; it opens about 90 degrees wide on a desk and 60 on a phone, centred on Orion; the Japanese page names the stars in katakana and the search finds a star by its katakana name.
- Every one of the 5,080 stars now has its `constellation` (`Star.constellation` is a `string`, no longer `string | undefined`): the catalogue's for the 2,736 with a Bayer letter or Flamsteed number, and the boundaries' for the other 2,344. `starsIn` therefore lists every star in a constellation, not only the lettered ones (Orion: 128 stars, not 73). `designation` is unchanged and is still `undefined` for a star with no letter or number.
- Fixed: a few Bayer letters were spelled in Latin and spaced from their number (`Xi 2 Cet`, `Nu 1 Dra`, `Mu Sco`, `Pi 3 Ori`; 66 stars). They are now Greek with the number joined, as every other star's: `ξ2 Cet`, `ν1 Dra`, `μ1 Sco`, `π3 Ori`, and found by those names. A name or designation typed the old way no longer finds the star.
- Fixed: `horizontalFrom` read its moment as TDB while everything else in the package takes UTC, so passing UTC put another planet's sky about 69 seconds early (0.28 degree on Mars). It now takes UTC and converts to Terrestrial Time inside, with the leap seconds from 1972 and Espenak and Meeus's delta T before; TDB minus TT (under 2 ms) is ignored. A caller who added the 69 seconds by hand must stop.
- A demo on GitHub Pages (https://johnmorrisdotca.github.io/hoshi-stars/): a sky chart you turn with a drag, zoom with the wheel or a pinch and search by name in English or Japanese; seven map projections, constellation lines and boundaries, the Messier objects, a faintest-star slider, a tap for a star's brightness, distance and colour, and "Put it in your page" code for HTML, ES modules, React, Vue, Angular, Svelte and an iframe of the embed page (`embed.html`, which draws what its address says). In English and Japanese, light and dark, at a phone's width and a desk's.
- New entry point `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/lines`: the stick figures of the 88 constellations (`figures`, `figureOf`), each a list of star pairs to join and the stars used, from about 2 kB of data. Our own figures, decided from the star data; every star of a line lies inside its constellation.
- New entry points `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/deep-sky` (the 110 Messier objects: `messier`, `deepSky`, `deepSkyObject`) and `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/deep-sky/ngc-ic` (the same functions over 11,898 Messier, NGC and IC objects). Positions, types and names from Wikidata (CC0), checked against NASA/IPAC NED; each object has its cross-identifications, type, constellation, magnitude and size where known.
- 25 more common star names (136 in all), among them Castor, Mira, Nunki, Sabik, Thuban, Alderamin, Sheliak and Sulafat, spelled as the IAU Working Group on Star Names spells them.
- New entry point `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/sky`: your sky. `horizontal` gives a star's altitude and azimuth (north through east) for a place and a moment, with optional refraction; also `isAboveHorizon`, `greenwichSiderealTime`, `localSiderealTime` and `precess` (J2000 to date). `horizontalFrom` does the same for a place on another planet from its IAU pole and prime meridian, passed in.
- New entry point `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/boundaries`: `constellationAt` says which of the 88 constellations a J2000 (or of-date) position is in, and `boundaryOf` and `boundaries` give the IAU boundaries as closed J2000 outlines to draw, with a point at least every degree along each edge. From N. G. Roman's table of Delporte's boundaries (VizieR VI/42), precessed to B1875.0; 8 of 8 of Roman's own test positions and all 2,736 bright stars that name a constellation agree.
- New entry point `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/constellations`: the 88 IAU constellations with abbreviation, Latin name and genitive, English name, and Japanese name (おおいぬ座) with its hiragana reading (`constellations`, `constellation`, `starsIn`).
- Map projections for star charts as their own import, `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/projection`: `project` and `unproject` for stereographic, orthographic, gnomonic, azimuthal equidistant, equirectangular, Mollweide and Hammer-Aitoff (`hammer`), with the sky's east-left convention, `mirror`, `rotation` and `scale`.
- First version: the 5,080 stars of the Yale Bright Star Catalogue to magnitude 6.0 with names, positions, proper motion, distance and colour (`stars`, `starByHr`, `starByName`, `starPosition`, `starDistanceLightYears`, `starTemperatureK`, `blackBodyColour`, `starColour`). Moved unchanged from wakusei-planets' `/stars`.
