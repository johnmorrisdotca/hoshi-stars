# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

- **Faint stars: no ghosts beside bright stars** (MODULES-769). UCAC4 holds detections in the halo of a bright star that are not stars: 516 of the 5,080 bright stars had a faint star within 20 arc-seconds. A star within 30 arc-seconds x 10^(0.2 x (5.5 - V)) of a bright star of magnitude V (at most 120) is now dropped unless a 2MASS point source confirms it (within 2 arc-seconds, no persistence, spike, stripe or halo flag, V - J from -1 to 4.5): of 857 stars in halos 182 are kept and 675 dropped, and 21 bright stars (from 516) have a faint star within 20 arc-seconds. UCAC4's object type 1 is not a ghost marker (3,873 of 4,000 are confirmed, as 3,835 of 4,000 ordinary stars are) and is not used. `make-faint-data.py download-ghost` fetches the 2MASS rows, `build --ghosts off` builds without the rule.
- **Faint stars: APASS DR10's magnitudes for the bright end** (MODULES-786). UCAC4's V of its stars brighter than about 9.5 ran 0.4 to 1.2 magnitudes too faint. 227,998 stars (V 7.0 to 10.2 by APASS DR10, south of declination +20.0, within 5 arc-seconds) now carry DR10's V, 155,858 of them its B-V; 64,528 are 0.25 magnitude or more brighter than before, 6,397 a magnitude or more. Between 6.5 and 8 the layer holds 7,838 stars (3,138 before) and between 7.5 and 9 77,770 (48,474). North of +20.0, where APASS DR10 has a tenth of its bright stars, nothing is corrected, and no blanket offset is applied (the guide says why). The tile format is unchanged; `faint-tiles/index.json` says per tile how many stars took APASS's V (`apassV.tiles`) and has the counts by quarter magnitude. `make-faint-data.py download-apass` fetches the DR10 rows of V 9.2 to 10.2, `build --correct apass,sao` is the hook for SAO's magnitudes (waits for NASA HEASARC, not in the tiles), `build --correct none --ghosts off` reproduces 0.18.1's tiles byte for byte. 2,461,864 stars in all (2,462,539 before); the tile set is 14.77 MB (14.78), 12.48 MB gzipped (12.47).
- **The constellation list starts at the top: compact filter chips, and it says which sky it is** (MODULES-754). The six filter rows (hemisphere, best month, made, how easy, asterisms, meteor showers) took about 60% of a desk screen before the first row. Now each filter is a small chip on one line that shows its choice (`Month: any`, `Month: Jan`, `Month: 2 chosen`) and opens a menu of its choices (a menu button: Down opens it, the arrows, Home and End move, Space or Enter ticks, Escape or a click outside closes it); the count and Clear all sit on the same line. On a phone one Filters button (with a badge counting the filters on) opens a bottom sheet with every filter as a row of buttons, a Show button with the count, Clear all and Close. Every choice is still kept in the address, in English and Japanese. The page says what it lists: the title is now "The IAU's 88 constellations (the Western sky)", with a link to the sky map for the Japanese and Chinese skies, because the table's columns (hemisphere, origin, difficulty, meteor showers) are facts of the 88 and have no counterpart for the other skies. The Japanese words await a native reader.

## [0.19.0] - 2026-11-09

- **Deep-sky objects at their true size, with pictures** (MODULES-610). New entry points `/deep-sky/shape` and `/deep-sky/ngc-ic-shape` (`deepSkyShape`, `deepSkyOutline`, `offsetOnSky`, `measuredParts`): the size, axis ratio and angle of a galaxy from RC3 (7,013 galaxies; its terms are being confirmed, and `--no-rc3` builds the shapes without it) and from NASA/IPAC NED's diameters table (2,706 more); 9,795 of the 11,898 objects have a size and 7,293 an angle, no angle is invented, and NGC 2000.0 and the Sky Catalog are not used. The outline is sky points for any projection. `/deep-sky/pictures` (`deepSkyPicture`, `deepSkyPicturesOf`, `deepSkyPictures`, `pictureCorners`, `pictureUrl`, `PICTURES_BASE`): an index of 130 pictures (112 survey pictures and 18 finer ESA/Hubble ones laid over them) of 113 objects (82 Messier objects and 31 brighter NGC and IC objects), made from SDSS (CC BY), 2MASS and WISE images cut by NASA's SkyView, so each lies at its exact place, and the Hubble ones (CC BY 4.0) placed by their own position tags; each is credited in `NOTICE.md`. **The picture files (9.5 MB, each under 90 kB) are not in the npm package**: they are served from the demo site, which `pictureUrl` uses by default, and `scripts/check-package.ts` fails if one enters the package. `/deep-sky/members` (`starsInside`): the catalogue stars inside a cluster's outline. The demo draws every object at its true size, growing with the zoom, lays the pictures only when zoomed in (fetched on demand, at most six at once), rings the bright stars inside an open cluster, keeps a new **Pictures** layer in the address (the **Messier** layer is now **Deep sky**, and draws the NGC and IC objects once zoomed in), and the card says what is measured, what is drawn and who is credited, in English and Japanese.

## [0.18.1] - 2026-11-09

- **The 6.5 to 8 gap, measured** (MODULES-660). Two ways to fill the faint layer's thin band were built and compared, and kept on file: the SAO Star Catalog (22,012 new stars in 6.5 to 8, the whole sky) and APASS DR10 with 2MASS positions (15,536, but almost none north of declination +20). ## [0.18.0] - 2026-11-09

- **A constellation in 3D** (MODULES-723). New entry point `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/constellation-3d`: `constellationIn3D(abbreviation, { from })` places the stars of a figure in space from their right ascension, declination and parallax, each with the nearest and farthest place its parallax error allows and the Earth's line of sight to it, from the Earth or from another star; `distanceQuality` and `distanceQualityTable` say how well each distance is known (good under 5% error, fair to 20%, rough above, none without a parallax: 25, 108, 370 and 90 of the 593 stars in the 88 figures); `positionIn3D` is the maths. A star with no parallax is never put at a guessed distance, and one with an error bar wider than its distance is a range, not a point. The demo has `constellation-3d.html` (also "See it in 3D" on each constellation's page, and a header link): the constellation as from the Earth, which is the sky chart's view exactly, then turned by a drag, the shared view controls or **Turn 90°** to see each star at its distance in light-years with its error bar, a faint streak for a wide range and a dotted ray for no distance, the Earth, a scale in light-years and labels that never overlap, from the Earth or from another star, in English and Japanese, light and dark; and an embed page `embed-3d.html` with `c`, `from`, `yaw`, `tilt`, `lang` and `theme`, and code for every way a page is made. This is the package's first WebGL: it draws through kazoku-ui's shared renderer (one context, only when something moves, at most 30 frames a second, canvas within 4096 pixels and 6 million, nothing when out of sight or in a hidden tab, three.js a dev dependency of the demo only), with `pnpm test:freeze` (run by CI and passed to the release as `freeze-command`), software drawing in every Chromium test project, and kazoku-ui 0.24.0's gesture check. EN/JA guide `docs/guides/constellation-in-3d.md`.

## [0.17.0] - 2026-11-09

- **Faint stars to magnitude 12 from UCAC4** (MODULES-655, 660). New entry point `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/faint`: 2,462,539 stars in 3,338 sky tiles (three levels by magnitude, 6 bytes a star, 14.8 MB, 12.6 MB gzipped) that a page loads for the patch of sky it shows: `faintSky` (loader, takes the `fetch` to use), `faintTilesFor`, `decodeFaintTile`, `faintStarsIn` (with a cap and the depth the list is complete to), `faintGrid`, `faintTileBounds`, `faintTilePath`, `FAINT_LEVELS`, `FAINT_LIMIT` and `FAINT_TILES_URL`. The tiles are `faint-tiles/` in the repository and on the Pages site, not in the npm package. `scripts/make-faint-data.py` downloads UCAC4 through NASA/IPAC IRSA's TAP service (outside the repository) and builds them. Only the rows UCAC4 measured itself: object types 4, 6 and 7 (copies from FK6, Hipparcos and Tycho-2) and Tycho's Vt and Bt are dropped, as are 45 duplicates of bright stars; the magnitude is APASS V where UCAC4 has one of its own, else its model magnitude. Brighter than about magnitude 9 it is thin (3,138 stars between 6.5 and 8). `NOTICE.md` has the licence sentences; the Faint stars guide (English and Japanese) the counts.
- **Filling the gap between the bright stars and UCAC4: built, compared, not merged yet** (MODULES-660). Between 6.5 and 8 the faint layer holds 3,138 stars where the sky holds about 33,600. Two fills were built from sources that measured their own stars, with scripts that download, build and compare them (`scripts/make-gap-sao.py`, `scripts/make-gap-apass.py`, `scripts/compare-gap.py`, shared code in `scripts/gap_common.py`) and a report (`docs/research/gap-6.5-8.md`, its numbers in `gap-6.5-8.json`): the SAO Star Catalog through NASA HEASARC (30,459 new stars from 6.0 to 9.0, 22,012 of them between 6.5 and 8, the whole sky, positions 1.1 arc-seconds from 2MASS at the median) and APASS DR10 with 2MASS positions (16,602 new stars, clean terms, but APASS has no bright stars north of declination +20). **Neither is in `faint-tiles/`**: SAO states no licence and NASA HEASARC is being asked to confirm its terms (NOTICE.md marks it "confirmation requested 2026-11-09"), and APASS leaves the north empty. `make-faint-data.py build` takes `--gap sao` (or `--gap apass-2mass,sao`) to merge one into level 0 when it is cleared; the default is none, which rebuilds the tiles byte for byte, and `index.json` now records the merge (`gapFill`). **A finding:** UCAC4's magnitudes of stars brighter than about 9.5 are 0.4 to 1.2 magnitudes too faint against APASS DR10 and SAO, which agree with each other; the guide says so. New `test/gap.test.ts`.
- **The demo's map draws them** (MODULES-655). A **Faint stars** layer, on by default and kept in the address, draws the faint stars of the field as zoom increases (the depth is the slider plus 2.5 magnitudes a tenfold zoom, up to 12): a few paths, never a node a star, at most 6,000 a view, brightest first; the line under the controls says `Stars to magnitude 9.5 here`. The embed page draws them too. Nothing is drawn or fetched at rest.

## [0.14.0] - 2026-11-09

- **A star's profile and portrait** (MODULES-719). Two new entry points. `/profile`: `starProfile(star)` works out a star's surface temperature (from its spectral type and class by published scales, or its colour), luminosity, radius (`R = sqrt(L) / T²` in solar units, or the typical radius of its type where its own distance is missing or too uncertain), apparent disc size (needs no distance) and limb-darkening coefficient, each with its uncertainty from the parallax, magnitude, temperature scale and bolometric correction; every one of the 5,080 stars has a temperature, a disc size and a limb darkening, 3,762 a radius, and where a figure is missing `unknown` says why. `starShape(hr)` gives the published shapes of Altair and Regulus (flattened) and Vega (seen over its pole). `/portrait`: `starPortrait` draws the star as SVG text from those numbers (disc in its temperature's colour, limb darkening, a generic surface texture of the right kind of cells, the measured flattened shape) and says so in the picture ("Drawn from its numbers, not a photo", "A generic look, not this star's surface"); `starComparison` draws it next to the Sun, with Earth's orbit for the supergiants; both in English and Japanese. Checked against 43 stars the CHARA Array resolved (the one-sigma range holds the measured disc for 33 of them; rms 0.94 sigma) and six famous ones; the figures that miss, and why, are in the guide. Sources and terms: NOTICE.md, "Stellar profiles". The demo's side panel shows the portrait, the star next to the Sun, and the figures with their ranges when a star is picked, and "What to embed" offers the star's picture with the usual code tabs and a new embed page, `star.html?star=2061&view=compare`. The distance in the panel now carries its error, and "Spectral class" is the whole type.

## [0.8.0] - 2026-11-09

- **The sky from another star** (MODULES-659). New entry points `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/sky-from` and `/sky-from/nearby`: `skyFrom(viewpoint, { magnitude, among })` moves every star with a distance to where it would be seen from a star, a name or a point `{ x, y, z }` in parsecs, and gives each one's right ascension and declination there, its distance and its apparent magnitude (`m' = M + 5 log10(d' / 10 pc)`), brightest first, with the Sun added (absolute magnitude 4.83): from Alpha Centauri it is a star of magnitude 0.45 at 2h40m, +60.8 degrees, in Cassiopeia. The viewpoint star is left out, and so is a star nearer to it than the error of its distance; the 2,517 bright stars with no parallax cannot be moved and are counted in `skipped`. `skyFromWithNearby` also moves the 5,565 nearby stars, in its own import (the plain one stays small; `index.js` is unchanged). Types `SeenStar`, `SeenSky`, `SkyFromOptions`, `SkyFromViewpoint`. The demo has a **View from** choice (the Earth's sky, Alpha Centauri, Sirius, Vega and more, or the star you picked) that redraws the chart, the constellation lines, the star facts and the Nearby panel from there, with a note of the distance and what is hidden, kept in the address as `from=HR5459`; the embed page, the code to copy and the iframe address carry it. The constellation boundaries are the Earth's directions and are not drawn from another star. `scripts/check-sky-from.py` checks the result against astropy (by hand, not in CI): they agree to 1e-9 arc-seconds. EN/JA guide `docs/guides/sky-from-another-star.md`.
- **`nearest(..., { by: "space" })` uses the bright stars' parallax errors** (MODULES-726). `error` is now worked out for a bright star, from `starDistance(star).lightYearsError`, whenever both ends have one (2,329 of the 2,563 bright stars with a distance do): Sirius to Procyon is 5.22 light-years, give or take 0.06. It is still `undefined` where a star has only the Bright Star Catalogue's parallax, which has no error. A target that is a point is taken as exact.
- **Zooming in shows fainter stars** (MODULES-716). The demo drew stars down to the slider's magnitude (5 by default) at every zoom, so a close view showed only a dozen stars. The faintest drawn now rises 2.5 magnitudes for each tenfold zoom, to the catalogue's faintest (6.0): at 25x round Sirius that is 37 stars instead of 12. The help says so in English and Japanese. The nearby stars and the NGC/IC layer when zoomed, and twinkling, are still to come.
- **The whole spectral type and a parallax with its error for every star** (MODULES-718, 710). `Star` gains `spectralType` (the catalogue's whole string: `M1-2Ia-Iab` for Betelgeuse, `A0Va` for Vega), `luminosityClass` (`Ia`, `Iab`, `Ib`, `I`, `II`, `III`, `IV`, `V`, `VI`, `D` or `undefined`: a supergiant, giant or dwarf), `parallaxErrorMas` and `parallaxSource` (`"GCTP"`, `"BSC"`, `"dynamical"`). `parallaxMas` is no longer a whole number: 2,329 stars take the Yale General Catalogue of Trigonometric Stellar Parallaxes' value to a tenth of a milliarcsecond with its error (Sirius 375 becomes 381.6 ± 2.2, so 8.5 light-years, not 8.7; Betelgeuse 5 becomes 9.8 ± 4.7; Vega 123 becomes 129.7 ± 1.6), the rest keep the Bright Star Catalogue's. `spectralClass` is unchanged. New `starDistance(star)` returns the distance with `lightYearsError` and `lightYearsRange`, and new `luminosityClass(spectralType)` reads the class from any type string; `NearbyStar` gains `luminosityClass` (Proxima Centauri is `V`). 30 more stars have a distance (2,563 of 5,080); the star table grows by 13.9 kB gzipped (108.2 to 122.0). The data files carry the new columns. Hipparcos (new reduction) is not used: ESA licenses it CC BY-NC 3.0 IGO (`NOTICE.md`).
- **Printable sheets** (MODULES-685, 687, 688). A new entry point `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/sheets`, pure SVG strings in millimetres: `dotsSvg` and `dotsFigure` (connect-the-dots constellations at three levels, dots sized by brightness, numbers in drawing order with a ring where the pencil is lifted, an answer page), `skyForLatitude` and `regionGuideSvg` (which constellations are always up, best this month or never seen at a latitude; Ursa Minor is never seen from Sydney, the Southern Cross never from Toronto), `starWheelDisc`, `starWheelPlate` and `starWheelPoster` (a planisphere for 20 to 70 degrees north or south, its window the horizon circle of the latitude, with a 400 mm poster version), and `skyMapSvg` (the evening sky of a month over a latitude). `kid` and `normal` types, English and Japanese. The demo has a new page `kids.html`: pick a sheet, a place (country and region from kuni, flags from hata, a latitude slider or your location when you ask) and a paper, see it in kazoku-ui's print layout and press Print / save PDF; the Pages build prints ready-made PDFs (`scripts/make-pdfs.mjs`). The package still has no runtime dependency (kuni, hata and kazoku-ui 0.22 are in the demo only). EN/JA guide `docs/guides/printable-sheets.md`.

## [0.5.0] - 2026-11-08

- New entry point `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/nearest`: `nearest(target, { count, within, kind, by, date, magnitude, among })` and `within(target, radius, options)` give the stars or Messier objects nearest a star, an object, a name or a point, nearest first, by angle on the sky (`{ thing, angle }`) or by distance in space from the parallaxes (`{ thing, distance, parsecs, error }`, with a `skipped` count; a point `{ x, y, z }` in parsecs is a target too), and `neighbouringConstellations(text)` gives the constellations that share a piece of boundary. One query over all 5,080 stars takes about 1 ms. The space answers cover only the bright-star catalogue until a nearby-star catalogue lands.
- The demo has a Nearby panel: pick a star, a Messier object or a constellation and it lists the ten nearest stars (in the sky, or in space for a star with a parallax) or the constellations that touch it, and rings them on the chart; `by=space` is kept in the address. The embed page does the same from `near=S:2491` (or `D:M31`, `C:Ori`) and `by=space`, and the code to copy carries them.

## [0.4.0] - 2026-11-08

- The demo's chart and the embed page take the family's hands (kazoku-ui's view controller): a drag turns the sky, two fingers pinch, Ctrl or Command and the wheel zoom about the pointer, `+`, `-`, the arrow keys and `0` work with the chart focused, and the double tap zooms. At the opening view the plain wheel scrolls the page, and a finger's up-and-down drag does too; zoomed in, the wheel and a drag move the sky. The embed page (`embed.html`) is no longer a still picture: it has the same hands and zoom in, zoom out and back-to-start buttons, and writes `ra`, `dec` and `zoom` into its own address.
- The zoom now runs from 0.1 (the whole sky, small) to 500 (about 0.7 degree across a desk-wide chart, where Mizar and Alcor still draw as two stars), up from 0.6 to 60. Lines and the Milky Way keep drawing at the top of the range: a point is placed out to 100,000 pixels from the middle (it was six times the chart's size), so a segment that crosses the chart is not lost. Constellation names are left out below 0.25, where they cannot be told apart.
- New npm keywords `react`, `galaxy` and `component`.

- The family's thumbnail, dark thumbnail and icon (`docs/images`, made by `pnpm images`): a sky chart of Orion with its lines on the Milky Way. The README opens with it and the demo carries it as its link card (og:image) and apple-touch-icon.
- `pnpm test:readme` runs every `js` and `ts` example of the README and the guides against the built package, and checks that they name every export and every picture exists. Run by CI and by the release.
- The demo keeps less empty room under its title on a phone. Fixed: the demo's chart failed to draw (an error in the console) once zoomed in past 5, with the Milky Way on. The getting-started guide said Orion has 12 lines to draw; it has 8.

## [0.3.0] - 2026-11-08

- 199 more common star names, 335 in all: every name on the IAU Catalog of Star Names whose star is in the 5,080, among them Acrux, Alcor, the Pleiades (Alcyone, Merope, Electra, Atlas, Maia, Taygeta, Pleione, Celaeno, Asterope), Rasalas, Mesarthim, Zosma and Tiaki. ## [0.2.0] - 2026-11-08

- New entry point `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/milky-way`: a brightness map of the Milky Way (about 11 kB of numbers) with the bright core toward Sagittarius and the Great Rift, the Coalsack and the Cygnus Rift as dimmer places. `milkyWayBrightness(point)` gives 0 to 1 toward a point of the sky, `milkyWayBands(levels, { step })` cuts it into ribbons of sky points to fill as soft bands, and `toGalactic` and `fromGalactic` change between right ascension and declination and galactic longitude and latitude. Made by `scripts/make-milky-way-data.py` from SAO star counts (NASA HEASARC; no licence stated, used with credit and removed on request), the IRAS 100 micron and COBE DIRBE dust maps (NASA, public domain) and a bulge of our own figures; see `NOTICE.md`.
- The demo: a **Milky Way** (天の川) button draws the band under the stars and the lines, in every projection, on by default; `layers=milky` is in the address and in the embed page's.

## [0.1.0] - 2026-11-08

- New `Star.nameJa`: the katakana name (`シリウス`, `ベテルギウス`) of each of the 136 named stars, which `starByName` also finds. The spellings are the IAU names written in Japanese letters by hand, with the usual Japanese forms; see `NOTICE.md`.
- The demo: a star's name or a constellation's is left out where it would sit on another label, bright stars first, and comes back when you zoom in; it opens about 90 degrees wide on a desk and 60 on a phone, centred on Orion; the Japanese page names the stars in katakana and the search finds a star by its katakana name.
- Every one of the 5,080 stars now has its `constellation` (`Star.constellation` is a `string`, no longer `string | undefined`): the catalogue's for the 2,736 with a Bayer letter or Flamsteed number, and the boundaries' for the other 2,344. `starsIn` therefore lists every star in a constellation, not only the lettered ones (Orion: 128 stars, not 73). `designation` is unchanged and is still `undefined` for a star with no letter or number.
- Fixed: a few Bayer letters were spelled in Latin and spaced from their number (`Xi 2 Cet`, `Nu 1 Dra`, `Mu Sco`, `Pi 3 Ori`; 66 stars). They are now Greek with the number joined, as every other star's: `ξ2 Cet`, `ν1 Dra`, `μ1 Sco`, `π3 Ori`, and found by those names. A name or designation typed the old way no longer finds the star.
- Fixed: `horizontalFrom` read its moment as TDB while everything else in the package takes UTC, so passing UTC put another planet's sky about 69 seconds early (0.28 degree on Mars). It now takes UTC and converts to Terrestrial Time inside, with the leap seconds from 1972 and Espenak and Meeus's delta T before; TDB minus TT (under 2 ms) is ignored. A caller who added the 69 seconds by hand must stop.
- A demo on GitHub Pages (https://johnmorrisdotca.github.io/hoshi-stars/): a sky chart you turn with a drag, zoom with the wheel or a pinch and search by name in English or Japanese; seven map projections, constellation lines and boundaries, the Messier objects, a faintest-star slider, a tap for a star's brightness, distance and colour, and "Put it in your page" code for HTML, ES modules, React, Vue, Angular, Svelte and an iframe of the embed page (`embed.html`, which draws what its address says). In English and Japanese, light and dark, at a phone's width and a desk's.
- New entry point `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/lines`: the stick figures of the 88 constellations (`figures`, `figureOf`), each a list of star pairs to join and the stars used, from about 2 kB of data. Our own figures, decided from the star data; every star of a line lies inside its constellation.
- New entry points `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/deep-sky` (the 110 Messier objects: `messier`, `deepSky`, `deepSkyObject`) and `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/deep-sky/ngc-ic` (the same functions over 11,898 Messier, NGC and IC objects). Positions, types and names from Wikidata (CC0), checked against NASA/IPAC NED; each object has its cross-identifications, type, constellation, magnitude and size where known.
- 25 more common star names (136 in all), among them Castor, Mira, Nunki, Sabik, Thuban, Alderamin, Sheliak and Sulafat, spelled as the IAU Working Group on Star Names spells them.
- New entry point `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/sky`: your sky. `horizontal` gives a star's altitude and azimuth (north through east) for a place and a moment, with optional refraction; also `isAboveHorizon`, `greenwichSiderealTime`, `localSiderealTime` and `precess` (J2000 to date). `horizontalFrom` does the same for a place on another planet from its IAU pole and prime meridian, passed in.
- New entry point `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/boundaries`: `constellationAt` says which of the 88 constellations a J2000 (or of-date) position is in, and `boundaryOf` and `boundaries` give the IAU boundaries as closed J2000 outlines to draw, with a point at least every degree along each edge. From N. G. Roman's table of Delporte's boundaries (VizieR VI/42), precessed to B1875.0; 8 of 8 of Roman's own test positions and all 2,736 bright stars that name a constellation agree.
- New entry point `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/constellations`: the 88 IAU constellations with abbreviation, Latin name and genitive, English name, and Japanese name (おおいぬ座) with its hiragana reading (`constellations`, `constellation`, `starsIn`).
- Map projections for star charts as their own import, `@johnmorrisdotca/hoshi-stars/projection`: `project` and `unproject` for stereographic, orthographic, gnomonic, azimuthal equidistant, equirectangular, Mollweide and Hammer-Aitoff (`hammer`), with the sky's east-left convention, `mirror`, `rotation` and `scale`.
- First version: the 5,080 stars of the Yale Bright Star Catalogue to magnitude 6.0 with names, positions, proper motion, distance and colour (`stars`, `starByHr`, `starByName`, `starPosition`, `starDistanceLightYears`, `starTemperatureK`, `blackBodyColour`, `starColour`). Moved unchanged from wakusei-planets' `/stars`.
