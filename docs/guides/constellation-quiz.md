[日本語](ja/constellation-quiz.md)

The demo's `quiz.html` shows a chart of the stars around one constellation with no name written on it, and six names in a row under it. Choose one: the right one turns green; a wrong one shakes (not with reduced motion) and the page says what it was. **Next question** asks another. The page keeps the score, the streak and, under the question, each constellation you missed with its picture and a link to its page, for as long as it is open.

- **Style** makes it easier or harder: *Plain* (the stars and nothing else, the hardest), *Stars and lines* (the constellation's figure as well) or *Annotated* (the figure, the IAU boundary, and the names of the neighbouring constellations, never the one asked).
- **Level** is which constellations it asks: *Bright 12* (the twelve whose figure stars give the most light), *Bright 30*, or *All 88*. The five wrong names are drawn from the same level.
- **Culture** asks about another sky: *Japanese* (the 88 constellations as Japan names and joins them, and its traditional star patterns, with at least three stars in the catalogue and two lines) or *Chinese mansions* (the Chinese sky's figures with at least three stars in the catalogue and two lines: the Twenty-Eight Mansions, then the star officials; see the Chinese levels below). The names are the data's own: Japanese or English for the Japanese sky (an old pattern with its reading, 昴 (すばる)); Traditional characters for the mansions, with the English name beside them where the data has one and the pinyin where it has not, and on the Japanese page Wikidata's Japanese label where it has one (two of them, 庫楼 and 文昌, corrected). Where it has none, only the characters of the Traditional name that have a standard Japanese form are written in that form (參 as 参, 攝 as 摂 and so on), so that the forms match and the answer cannot be told by the shape of a character (婁, 畢, 觜, 軫, 張 and 氐 are the same characters in Japanese, so they stay as they are). The levels are that sky's own, the chart draws that culture's lines, *Annotated* is not offered (its borders and neighbours are the Western sky's), and the address keeps `culture` (`japanese` or `chinese`; Western is the default).
- **The Chinese levels** are, in order: *Bright 12* and *All 21*, the Twenty-Eight Mansions with at least three of their stars in the catalogue (the other 7 of the 28 hold fewer); *Small mansions (7)*, those 7 asked the other way round (see below); *Purple Forbidden (14)* and *Heavenly Market (8)*, the star officials whose enclosure Wikidata names; then *Star officials 1* to *4* (24 each), all the other star officials, the brightest first. Every star official is in one level and the five wrong names come from the same level. The Supreme Palace has only two such figures, too few for the six names, so they are with the officials in the tranches (as is the one that is part of a mansion). A figure whose name another asterism has too (the old lists have two 五諸侯) is not asked, because neither could be the right one to choose. **Small mansions** (角, 氐, 虛, 室, 壁, 婁 and 觜, each with one or two of its stars in the catalogue, too few for a figure) ask "Which mansion holds the ringed star?": a chart of a fixed field (a point 20 degrees from the middle falls 42 per cent of the short side from the middle: about 48 degrees across the short side), centred on the middle of the mansion's held stars, with those stars, and no others, ringed, and the stars of the other mansions drawn faintly round them (with their lines in the *Stars and lines* style). The six names are the right mansion and five others of the 28, the nearest in the sky first. It is a level of its own, not mixed into *All 21*, because the question and the chart are not the same and the old addresses keep their meaning. The ring never says "determinative": 氐 and 觜 have no star numbered 1 in the data and the Wikidata numbers are not relied on. The enclosure of a star official is what Wikidata says it is part of (`partOf` in the data, from the dump `data-sources/chinese-constellations-wikidata.jsonl`, read 2026-11-17): the dump gave no enclosure that the data did not already have, so the levels have the same figures (the Supreme Palace still has two). On the Japanese page a mansion or official that Wikidata has a Japanese label for shows it (虚宿, 参宿; 38 of them, as Wikidata gives them but for two corrections, 庫楼 and 文昌, awaiting a native reader), and the others show the Traditional name with the same characters in their Japanese form. The address keeps `level` (`12`, `88`, `small-mansions`, `purple-forbidden`, `heavenly-market` or `officials-1` to `officials-4`); the old `level=88` of the Chinese sky is still the 21 mansions, so every older link asks what it asked. A level the sky chosen leaves with fewer than six figures (the northern Purple Forbidden from 40 degrees south) is not offered, and one in an address becomes *All*.
- **Sky** limits the quiz to what a reader can see: a **latitude** (type a whole number of degrees from -90 to 90, north positive; a decimal is rounded, and a number outside the range is refused and the one before is kept) or *My location*, and a month, or *Any month* for the whole year. A constellation counts when more than half of the stars of its figure are more than 10 degrees above the horizon at 21:00 on the 15th of the month, worked out with the package's `horizontal`; the Southern Cross is never asked in the north, Orion not on a July evening. The level is then taken from that sky, and the six names come from it. The address keeps `lat` (a whole number of degrees) and `month` (1 to 12); the old `hemisphere=north` or `south` of earlier links still opens, as 40 and -40, and when both are there `lat` wins. Empty is the whole sky. The levels that this sky leaves with too few figures are not offered, as before.
  **My location** asks your device where you are only when you press it, and uses only the latitude, rounded to a whole degree. Your position is not kept: it is not written to the address (not even the rounded latitude, so a copied link or the code for a frame does not carry it, the same as the Tonight and My sky pages), not stored and not sent anywhere. Reload and it is gone; type the latitude to keep it in the address. If you refuse, or the device cannot tell, the page says so and keeps the latitude it had.
- **Put it in your page**: the panel under the quiz gives the code for a frame, `<iframe src=".../embed-quiz.html?level=88&style=lines&kid=1">`, at 320, 480 or 640 wide. The embed page is the quiz without its controls: its address chooses `style`, `level`, `kid=1`, `lat` (or the old `hemisphere`), `month` and `lang=ja`, it writes nothing back to its address, and the question is never in the code.
- The five wrong names are the ones easy to mix up with the answer: up to three of its neighbours in the sky, then constellations made in the same years, then the rest of the level. The arrow keys move across the six names (up and down by a row), and 1 to 6 answer.
- **Kid mode** (a button beside the level) makes every button at least 56 px tall, the six names bigger still, and says things more simply ("Which star picture is this?", "Yes! It is Orion!", *Easy*, *Medium*, *Hard*). It is kept in the address as `kid=1`, and it never names the answer before you have chosen.
- The address keeps `style` (`lines` or `annotated`; plain is the default), `level` (`30` or `88`; the bright 12 is the default) and `q`, the question: the constellation's place in the list of the 88, from 1 (`quiz.html?q=60&level=88`), so a reload or a shared link asks the same question (with the same answer, but its five wrong names are drawn again). The address never has the constellation's name, but it is not a secret: a number can be looked up. A hand-written `c=Ori` is read too, to ask a chosen constellation. It is a quiz for the classroom and the family, not a test.

The chart is the package's own `chart()` drawn once for each question, centred on `centreOfConstellation` and zoomed to fit the figure. Nothing moves and nothing is drawn while you think. The keys 1 to 6 choose a name, and the focus goes to **Next question** after an answer and to the first name after that. It works on a phone, the six names in two or three columns, and the names are in Japanese on the Japanese page.
